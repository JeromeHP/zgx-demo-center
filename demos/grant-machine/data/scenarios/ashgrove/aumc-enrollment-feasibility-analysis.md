## Enrollment Feasibility Analysis — Proposed Study

**Project Title:** Stepped Mobilization and Sedation Minimization in Critical Illness (SMSM)
**Principal Investigator:** Dr. Saoirse Achterberg, Associate Professor of Pulmonary and Critical Care Medicine
**Institution:** Ashgrove University Medical Center (Ashgrove)

### Executive Summary of Recruitment Strategy

The Stepped Mobilization and Sedation Minimization in Critical Illness (SMSM) study is designed as a five-year multicenter trial requiring the activation of three primary clinical sites. This analysis confirms that the proposed recruitment targets are achievable within the specified timeline, leveraging Ashgrove’s extensive infrastructure and the established commitment of its affiliated hospital network. The institutional capacity to support this investigation is robust, anchored by a faculty body of 980 physicians and an active portfolio of 412 concurrent clinical trials. With an annual research expenditure of $268 million, Ashgrove University Medical Center possesses the financial and operational resources necessary to sustain complex critical care research without compromising existing services.

### Site Capacity and EHR-Derived Eligibility Projections

To ensure realistic accrual projections, we utilized electronic health record (EHR) screening data from the three proposed sites: Ashgrove University Medical Center (main campus), Wrenfield Regional Hospital, and Calloway Valley Health. Formal letters of collaboration have been secured from Wrenfield Regional Hospital (dated August 9, 2026) and Calloway Valley Health (dated August 21, 2026), confirming their readiness to activate for the SMSM protocol.

Our eligibility criteria focus on mechanically ventilated adult patients in the intensive care unit who meet specific physiological thresholds for early mobilization. Screening of the EHR database across these three sites over the preceding twelve months identified a consistent monthly volume of potentially eligible candidates. After applying exclusion criteria—specifically those related to severe baseline neurological injury, hemodynamic instability precluding movement, and contraindications to sedation reduction—the net pool of screen-eligible patients is estimated at approximately 180 to 200 individuals per site per month.

This EHR-derived eligibility pool provides a substantial buffer against typical screening failures. The projected accrual rate is set at 20 participants per site per month. Given the identified eligible volume, this target represents a conversion rate of less than 10% from screen-eligible to consented participant. This conservative estimate accounts for clinical workflow disruptions, family decision-making timelines, and the rigorous informed consent process required for critically ill populations.

### Screening-to-Consent Ratio and Operational Feasibility

The projected accrual of 20 participants per site per month relies on an expected screening-to-consent ratio of approximately 5:1 to 6:1. This ratio is derived from internal pilot assessments and historical EHR data patterns specific to the SMSM inclusion criteria. It is important to note that these projections are based solely on current EHR screening counts for the proposed study parameters; they do not rely on comparative analysis with prior trial outcomes but rather on the immediate, available population health data within the Ashgrove network.

At Ashgrove University Medical Center, the high density of faculty physicians (980) and the centralized nature of the main campus facilitate efficient identification of eligible patients. The EHR system is configured to flag potential candidates automatically, reducing the manual burden on research coordinators. At Wrenfield Regional Hospital and Calloway Valley Health, local clinical teams have been trained in the specific nuances of the SMSM protocol, ensuring that screening accuracy remains high.

The feasibility of meeting the 20-participant monthly target is further supported by the integrated structure of Ashgrove’s four-hospital system. The shared electronic health record platform allows for real-time monitoring of patient eligibility across all three sites. This interoperability enables rapid identification of patients who meet the stepped mobilization criteria, minimizing the lag time between clinical presentation and research screening.
