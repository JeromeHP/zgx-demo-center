## Data Management and Sharing Plan

Ashgrove University Medical Center (“Ashgrove”) is committed to the rigorous stewardship of clinical data generated under the proposed project, *Stepped Mobilization and Sedation Minimization in Critical Illness* (SMSM). As an academic medical center and affiliated university health system serving a four-hospital system in the Mid-Atlantic region, Ashgrove maintains robust infrastructure for research administration and data integrity. This plan outlines the strategies for managing, preserving, and sharing data collected during the five-year project period, beginning July 1, 2027. Our approach aligns with NIH guidelines and leverages our existing institutional frameworks to ensure transparency, reproducibility, and participant privacy.

### Data Types and Collection Methods

The SMSM study will generate a comprehensive dataset encompassing clinical, physiological, and operational metrics from critically ill patients across Ashgrove’s four active hospital sites. Primary data types include electronic health record (EHR) extracts, vital sign monitoring streams, ventilator settings, sedation administration logs, and mobility assessment scores. Secondary data will include demographic information, comorbidity indices, and adverse event reports. Data collection instruments have been standardized across all participating sites to ensure uniformity. Given Ashgrove’s status as a major research institution with 412 active clinical trials and an annual research expenditure of $268 million, we utilize validated Case Report Forms (CRFs) integrated directly into our institutional EHR systems to minimize transcription errors and enhance real-time data quality control.

### De-identification Approach

To protect participant confidentiality in accordance with HIPAA regulations and NIH policies, all shared data will undergo rigorous de-identification prior to release. Ashgrove’s Office of Clinical Research Administration, led by Director Lenore Kasprzak, oversees the de-identification protocol. We employ a hybrid approach combining automated software scripts and manual review by certified privacy officers. Direct identifiers (e.g., names, MRNs, dates of birth) are removed or hashed using one-way encryption algorithms established by Ashgrove’s IT Security Division. Indirect identifiers are generalized or suppressed where necessary to prevent re-identification risks. The resulting dataset will be stripped of any information that could reasonably link the data back to specific individuals at Wrenfield Regional Hospital, Calloway Valley Health, or other collaborating sites.

### Repository and Controlled Access

De-identified SMSM data will be deposited in the NIH Data Management and Sharing (DMS) compliant repository, the Database of Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (DSMA) or an equivalent NIH-approved institutional repository such as the NIH Genotypes and Phenotypes (dbGaP) database, depending on final funder guidance. Given the clinical nature of the data, we anticipate submission to dbGaP, which provides a secure environment for controlled-access health data. Access will be governed by a Data Use Agreement (DUA). Researchers seeking access must submit an application detailing their proposed use, demonstrating ethical approval, and agreeing to the terms of the DUA, which prohibit attempts to re-identify participants or share data with unauthorized third parties. The Office of Clinical Research Administration will manage the application review process in coordination with Ashgrove’s Institutional Review Board (IRB).

### Consent Language Supporting Sharing

The informed consent process for SMSM explicitly addresses data sharing. Participants will be provided with clear, plain-language documentation explaining that their de-identified clinical data may be shared with qualified researchers outside the study team for secondary analysis. The consent form states: *“Your health information may be used in future research studies. To protect your privacy, we will remove personal details like your name and address before sharing any data. However, please note that even after these details are removed, there is a small chance someone might figure out who you are if they combine this information with other public records. You can choose to opt out of data sharing if you wish.”* This language ensures transparency and respects participant autonomy while facilitating open science initiatives.

### Timelines and Responsibilities

Data management activities will follow a structured timeline aligned with the project’s five-year duration (2027–2032). During Years 1–2, Dr. Saoirse Achterberg (Principal Investigator) and Dr. Vikram Ostrowski-Baird (Co-PI, Director of Clinical Trials Biostatistics Unit) will oversee data collection protocols and initial cleaning. By the end of Year 2, a pilot dataset will be prepared for internal quality assurance. Data finalization and de-identification will occur in Years 4–5, coinciding with the primary completion phase of earlier related efforts (e.g., prior award AUMC-FICT-R01-2021, which concluded primary data collection in June 2025). Submission to the controlled-access repository is scheduled for Month 6 post-primary completion (Year 6), allowing time for peer-reviewed publications (targeting at least six papers, based on prior successful grants) and internal audits. Dr. Yusuf Brandolini, Chief Research Officer, provides overarching oversight, ensuring compliance with all institutional and federal mandates throughout the lifecycle of the SMSM project.
