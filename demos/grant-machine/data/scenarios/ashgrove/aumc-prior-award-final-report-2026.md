## Executive Summary and Award Overview

This report serves as the Final Progress Report for the NIH National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI) award identified as AUMC-FICT-R01-2021, titled *Early Mobilization Protocols in Mechanically Ventilated Patients*. The primary objective of this five-year trial was to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and feasibility of implementing standardized early mobilization protocols for adult patients receiving invasive mechanical ventilation. Sponsored by Ashgrove University Medical Center (Ashgrove), an academic medical centre and affiliated university health system serving a four-hospital system in the Mid-Atlantic region, this project operated under the leadership of Principal Investigator Dr. Saoirse Achterberg, Associate Professor of Pulmonary and Critical Care Medicine, with co-investigator Dr. Vikram Ostrowski-Baird serving as Director of the Clinical Trials Biostatistics Unit.

The award period spanned from July 1, 2021, to June 30, 2026, with a total funding amount of $2,640,000. The study was registered under ID NCT-FICT-2021-014, with primary completion targeted for June 30, 2025, and final results due by the award end date. As of today, September 21, 2026, the trial has concluded its enrollment and intervention phases. While the study successfully generated significant scientific output and established robust operational frameworks for future critical care research, it fell short of several key quantitative targets established in the original protocol. This document provides an honest accounting of those outcomes, detailing both the achievements and the specific shortfalls that characterized the trial’s execution.

## Enrollment and Site Activation Outcomes

The most significant deviation from the original study plan involved participant enrollment and site activation. The protocol originally anticipated enrolling 450 participants across seven planned clinical sites. However, final data confirms that only 288 participants were enrolled, representing a shortfall of 162 individuals against the target. Furthermore, while seven sites were initially identified for participation, only four of these seven sites were successfully activated and contributed data to the trial.

The reasons for these shortfalls are multifactorial and were documented throughout the study period. The primary driver of the reduced site count was operational instability during the start-up phase. Two of the originally planned sites withdrew from the consortium during the initial recruitment window due to internal staffing realignments and an inability to secure sufficient research nursing support. This withdrawal occurred before these sites could achieve full activation status, thereby reducing the geographic diversity and patient pool accessibility that had been projected.

The lower-than-projected screening yield at the remaining four active sites was attributed to several intersecting factors. First, the global clinical landscape during the latter half of the study period introduced variable ICU admission rates that differed from the baseline assumptions used in the grant application. Second, stricter institutional review board (IRB) interpretations regarding informed consent for mechanically ventilated patients led to longer screening windows and higher exclusion rates. Despite these challenges, the enrolled cohort of 288 participants remains a substantial dataset, providing adequate statistical power for primary outcome analysis, though it limits the generalizability of findings across a broader, more diverse critical care population than originally envisioned.

## Scientific Output and Data Integrity

Despite the enrollment shortfalls, the project achieved notable success in disseminating its findings and maintaining rigorous scientific standards. The research team produced six peer-reviewed papers detailing various aspects of early mobilization, including sub-analyses of patient mobility trajectories, cost-effectiveness models, and long-term functional outcomes. These publications have been distributed across high-impact journals in critical care medicine and pulmonary health, contributing significantly to the body of evidence supporting early mobilization practices.

Data integrity was maintained through close collaboration with Harbrace Clinical Monitoring, whose lead monitor, Dr. Ilse Nakagawa-Reilly, conducted regular oversight visits. The trial reported 14 protocol deviations over its five-year duration. These deviations were primarily related to timing variances in the initiation of mobilization sessions and minor documentation errors in vital sign recording during active therapy. None of the deviations compromised patient safety or invalidated the primary endpoint data. The Clinical Trials Biostatistics Unit, under Dr. Ostrowski-Baird’s direction, performed rigorous intent-to-treat analyses that accounted for these deviations, ensuring that the reported outcomes reflect a conservative and accurate estimation of the intervention’s effect.

## Financial Administration and Resource Utilization

The financial administration of Award AUMC-FICT-R01-2021 was managed by Lenore Kasprzak, Director of the Office of Clinical Research Administration at Ashgrove. The institution applied its approved fringe rate agreement of 31.2% (documented in aumc-rate-agreement-fy2026) and an indirect cost rate of 62.5% on Modified Total Direct Costs (MTDC). The total expenditure remained within the $2,640,000 ceiling, with careful monitoring of burn rates to ensure funds were utilized efficiently despite the need for additional monitoring costs associated with the remote oversight of withdrawing sites.

The successful completion of this trial has informed the design of a new project, *Stepped Mobilization and Sedation Minimization in Critical Illness* (SMSM). Ashgrove is currently pursuing funding for SMSM under NIH Solicitation PA-25-305. Letters of support have been secured from key collaborators, including Wrenfield Regional Hospital (dated August 9, 2026) and Calloway Valley Health (dated August 21, 2026), demonstrating sustained institutional commitment to advancing critical care mobilization research. The budget summary for the proposed SMSM project requests $3,706,990 over five years, reflecting lessons learned from the prior award regarding site management and cost structures.

## Conclusion and Future Directions

The *Early Mobilization Protocols in Mechanically Ventilated Patients* trial has concluded its active phase. While the failure to activate all planned sites and the enrollment shortfall relative to the 450-participant target present clear operational challenges, the project has delivered valuable scientific insights through six peer-reviewed publications and a robust dataset of 288 participants. The 14 reported protocol deviations were managed without compromising data validity or patient safety.

Ashgrove University Medical Center remains committed to advancing critical care research. The findings from this trial will directly inform the methodology of the upcoming SMSM study, particularly in refining site activation strategies and improving screening efficiency. The results of this award are currently being prepared for posting, with results due by June 30, 2026, as per the original award terms. We anticipate that the data generated will serve as a critical reference point for future NIH-funded investigations into early mobilization in intensive care settings.
