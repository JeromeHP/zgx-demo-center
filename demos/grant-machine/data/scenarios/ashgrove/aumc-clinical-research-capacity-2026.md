## Clinical Research Capacity and Infrastructure

Ashgrove University Medical Center (Ashgrove), an academic medical centre and affiliated university health system serving the Mid-Atlantic region through a four-hospital network, possesses robust infrastructure to support complex clinical trials. With a total of 1,140 hospital beds and a faculty of 980 physicians, Ashgroge provides a substantial patient base for recruitment and retention. The institution currently manages 412 active clinical trials, supported by an annual research expenditure of $268,000,000. This extensive operational framework ensures that new studies, such as the proposed *Stepped Mobilization and Sedation Minimization in Critical Illness* (SMSM), can be executed with high fidelity to protocol standards and regulatory requirements.

### Research Pharmacy and Clinical Operations

The Ashgrove Research Pharmacy operates as a centralized hub for investigational product management, ensuring strict adherence to Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) and institutional safety protocols. The pharmacy staff is trained in the handling, storage, dispensing, and accountability of all investigational agents, including those requiring cold chain logistics or complex reconstitution procedures critical for the SMSM study’s mobilization interventions.

Clinical research coordination is overseen by a dedicated team led by Lenore Kasprzak, Director of the Office of Clinical Research Administration. Under her supervision, clinical research coordinators manage daily trial operations, including informed consent processes, data entry, and participant follow-up. The office ensures seamless integration between the clinical teams and administrative bodies, facilitating efficient study start-up and compliance monitoring. Dr. Saoirse Achterberg, Associate Professor of Pulmonary and Critical Care Medicine and Principal Investigator for the SMSM project, provides direct scientific leadership, ensuring that the clinical methodology aligns with current standards in pulmonary care.

### Clinical Research Unit and Inpatient Capacity

Ashgrove’s Clinical Research Unit (CRU) serves as the primary site for inpatient trial activities, offering dedicated beds specifically designed for research protocols requiring close monitoring. The CRU is equipped with advanced physiological monitoring systems, telemetry capabilities, and immediate access to diagnostic imaging and laboratory services. This infrastructure is particularly vital for the SMSM study, which involves stepped mobilization protocols in critically ill patients. The unit’s staffing model includes specialized nursing personnel trained in critical care research, ensuring that participants receive both clinical care and research-specific interventions safely.

The four-hospital system allows for distributed recruitment sites while maintaining centralized oversight. Ashgrove maintains a state-of-the-art biospecimen processing core facility equipped with automated aliquoting systems, ultra-low temperature freezers, and quality control protocols for sample integrity. Typical study start-up timelines from protocol approval to first patient enrolled average 12–14 weeks for phase III trials, contingent upon site activation status.
