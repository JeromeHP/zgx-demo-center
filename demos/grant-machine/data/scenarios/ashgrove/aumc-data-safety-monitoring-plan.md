## Data and Safety Monitoring Plan

Ashgrove University Medical Center is committed to ensuring the highest standards of participant safety and data integrity for the proposed study, *Stepped Mobilization and Sedation Minimization in Critical Illness* (SMSM). As an academic medical center with a robust infrastructure supporting 412 active clinical trials and an annual research expenditure of $268,000,000, Ashgrove maintains rigorous oversight mechanisms for all human subjects research. This Data and Safety Monitoring Plan (DSMP) outlines the monitoring frequency, adverse event reporting protocols, and the composition of the Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) intended to oversee this multi-site trial involving Wrenfield Regional Hospital and Calloway Valley Health.

### Monitoring Frequency and Oversight Structure

The primary investigator, Dr. Saoirse Achterberg, Associate Professor of Pulmonary and Critical Care Medicine, retains ultimate responsibility for the conduct of the study. To support this oversight, the Office of Clinical Research Administration, led by Grants Director Lenore Kasprzak, will facilitate regular compliance reviews. Monitoring activities will be conducted at quarterly intervals during the first year of the trial, commencing on July 1, 2027, and transitioning to semi-annual reviews thereafter, unless the DSMB recommends more frequent assessments based on interim findings.

The Clinical Trials Biostatistics Unit, under the direction of Co-Investigator Dr. Vikram Ostrowski-Baird, will perform ongoing data audits. Given Ashgrove’s experience with prior NIH-funded research, such as the Early Mobilization Protocols in Mechanically Ventilated Patients (NCT-FICT-2021-014), which successfully enrolled 288 participants across four sites, we apply similar rigorous statistical monitoring techniques. Independent third-party monitoring will be contracted through Harbrace Clinical Monitoring, with Dr. Ilse Nakagawa-Reilly serving as the lead monitor. This external review ensures objectivity and adherence to Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines throughout the five-year duration of the project.

### Adverse Event Reporting Pathway and Timelines

In accordance with federal regulations and NIH policy, all adverse events (AEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs) will be reported through a centralized electronic safety reporting system integrated into Ashgrove’s institutional review board (IRB) platform. The reporting pathway is designed to minimize delay and maximize clarity:

1.  **Immediate Reporting:** Site investigators must report any SAE within 24 hours of awareness to the Principal Investigator (Dr. Saoirse Achterberg) and the local IRB.
2.  **Institutional Notification:** Ashgrove’s Office of Clinical Research Administration will review all SAE reports within 48 hours for completeness and regulatory compliance.
3.  **DSMB Submission:** Summarized safety data, including unblinded treatment assignments, will be submitted to the DSMB at each scheduled interim analysis. Unblinded individual participant data will only be accessible to designated DSMB members under strict confidentiality agreements.

For non-serious AEs, a cumulative summary will be provided to the IRB and funding agency (NIH) as part of the annual progress report. The study team will adhere to the timeline established in the protocol, ensuring that any emerging safety signals are evaluated by the DSMB within seven days of identification.

### Composition of the Data Safety Monitoring Board

The SMSM trial will be overseen by an independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB). It is important to state clearly that this board has not yet been convened and that the members listed below are proposed rather than appointed. Final appointment of DSMB members will occur no later than 30 days after grant award, subject to NIH approval and institutional conflict-of-interest clearance.

The proposed DSMB will consist of five experts representing diverse disciplines relevant to critical care, biostatistics, and ethics:

*   **Chair:** Dr. Elena Rostova, Professor of Critical Care Medicine at a peer institution, with extensive experience in sedation minimization trials.
*   **Biostatistician:** Dr. Vikram Ostrowski-Baird will not serve on the DSMB to avoid conflict of interest; instead, Dr. Julian Thorne, an independent biostatistician from the Mid-Atlantic region, is proposed as a member.
*   **Pulmonary/Critical Care Specialist:** A senior attending physician from Wrenfield Regional Hospital, ensuring site-specific clinical perspective.
*   **Ethics/Law Expert:** Dr. Sarah Jenkins, bioethicist specializing in vulnerable populations in intensive care settings.
*   **Patient Advocate:** A representative from the Calloway Valley Health patient advisory council, providing community-centric insight.

The DSMB will meet virtually every six months. Its charter grants the board full authority to recommend continuation, modification, or termination of the study based on safety data. This structure leverages Ashgrove’s existing network of collaborators and ensures that the SMSM trial benefits from both institutional expertise and independent external oversight, mirroring the successful governance frameworks used in our prior NIH R01 awards.
