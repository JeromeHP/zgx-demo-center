## Institutional Context and Evaluation Scope

As the Office of Institutional Research at Tierra Sandoval University, we recognize our mandate to provide rigorous, data-driven assessment of institutional initiatives. Our work plan for evaluating the proposed Bridging Computing Pathways for Rural Transfer Students (BCP) scholarship programme is grounded in our institution’s identity as a Hispanic-Serving Institution with 9,420 total enrollees. With a student body where 63% are first-generation college students and 58% receive Pell Grants, we prioritize metrics that reflect equity and access. The BCP programme, running for six years from August 1, 2027, to July 31, 2033, will serve 11 scholars annually under NSF S-STEM Solicitation 25-514 (Track 2). Our evaluation framework is designed to track the efficacy of this intervention within our specific service area in the Inland Southwest, ensuring that our findings contribute directly to the mission of Dr. Esperanza Quintanilla-Boyd, President of Tierra Sandoval, and support the strategic goals outlined by Associate Provost for Student Success, Dr. Nayeli Arbuthnot.

## Analytical Resources and Personnel Allocation

To execute this evaluation, we have allocated 5% effort from a dedicated senior analyst within our office. This resource will be responsible for data extraction, longitudinal tracking, and statistical analysis throughout the life of the award. The analyst will work in close coordination with the BCP Principal Investigator, Dr. Nayeli Arbuthnot, and Co-Principal Investigator Dr. Elias Vorderman, Chair of the Department of Computer Information Systems. Additionally, we will liaise regularly with Marisela Thorncroft, Director of Grants and Contracts, to ensure that evaluation activities align with federal compliance requirements and internal fiscal reporting standards. This dedicated effort ensures that data collection does not burden faculty teaching loads while maintaining high fidelity in our institutional records.

## Formative and Summative Measures

Our evaluation strategy employs a mixed-methods approach, utilizing both formative and summative measures to capture the full scope of student success. We will track three primary outcome metrics: retention rates, transfer completion status, and time to degree. These indicators are critical given our prior experience with the NSF Improving Undergraduate STEM Education: HSI Program award (TSU-FICT-HSI-2022), "Pathways to Computing Degrees for Transfer Students," which ran from September 1, 2022, to August 31, 2025. That prior initiative served 214 students and resulted in 61 transfer completions, providing a baseline against which we will measure the BCP cohort’s progress.

Formative assessment will occur continuously through mid-year checks, monitoring scholar engagement with faculty mentorship and professional development hours. Summative evaluation will be conducted at the end of each academic year and upon programme completion. We will also assess the impact of our collaboration with Cordillera Basin Community College and the Saltbush Regional Workforce Board, leveraging the articulation agreements signed during the prior cycle and the five additional agreements currently in planning stages. By comparing BCP scholars’ outcomes against a matched control group of non-scholar transfer students from our partner institutions, we will isolate the programme’s specific contribution to student persistence and graduation.

## Data Sources and FERPA Compliance

The evaluation will draw exclusively on verified institutional data sources. Primary records include student information systems (SIS) for demographic and enrollment data, learning management system logs for engagement metrics, and financial aid records for Pell Grant status verification. We will also utilize outcomes data from the prior award cycle, documented in our internal repository as `tsu-prior-outcomes-data`, to establish historical trends. All data handling strictly adheres to the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA). Personally identifiable information (PII) will be de-identified at the point of extraction. Our analyst will use encrypted servers for storage, with access limited to authorized personnel involved in the evaluation. No aggregate data will be released to external parties without prior approval from the Office of Institutional Research and legal counsel.

## Reporting Calendar and Dissemination

We commit to a structured reporting schedule to keep stakeholders informed. Quarterly progress reports will be submitted to the BCP programme team, focusing on formative indicators such as scholar engagement and early retention signals. Biannual summative reports will be delivered to the Provost’s office and Dr. Arbuthnot, detailing comparative analysis of retention and completion rates against our baseline. An annual evaluation summary will be shared with the S-STEM project officer at NSF, aligned with the fiscal year ending July 31. Finally, a comprehensive final report will be compiled upon programme conclusion in 2033, synthesizing all data to inform future HSI designation cycles and institutional strategy. This framework ensures transparency, accountability, and continuous improvement in our efforts to support rural transfer students in computing disciplines.
