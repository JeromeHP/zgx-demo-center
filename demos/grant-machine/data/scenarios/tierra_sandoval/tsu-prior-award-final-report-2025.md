## Executive Summary and Institutional Context

Tierra Sandoval University, a public regional comprehensive university and designated Hispanic-Serving Institution located in the Inland Southwest, submits this final report for the NSF Improving Undergraduate STEM Education: HSI Program award (TSU-FICT-HSI-2022). The project, titled "Pathways to Computing Degrees for Transfer Students," was active from September 1, 2022, through August 31, 2025. This initiative was designed to address the persistent attrition rates among transfer students entering computing programs, a critical demographic within our service area where 63% of our student body is first-generation and 58% are Pell Grant recipients.

Under the leadership of Principal Investigator Dr. Nayeli Arbuthnot, Associate Provost for Student Success, and Co-Principal Investigator Dr. Elias Vorderman, Chair of the Department of Computer Information Systems, the project sought to create robust support structures for transfer students pursuing degrees in computing disciplines. The total funding provided was $449,800, managed in coordination with Marisela Thorncroft, Director of Grants and Contracts. The outcomes detailed below reflect both the successes achieved in direct student services and curriculum development, as well as specific challenges encountered regarding institutional partnerships.

## Project Outcomes and Deliverables

The project successfully met its primary goals related to direct student intervention and faculty capacity building. Over the three-year period, the initiative served a total of 214 transfer students who were either newly admitted or currently enrolled in computing-related coursework at Tierra Sandoval. Of these participants, 61 students successfully completed their transfer pathways and graduated with a degree in a computing field during the award period. This completion rate represents a significant achievement given the complex logistical and academic hurdles often faced by transfer populations.

A core component of the project was the enhancement of curricular relevance and accessibility. The team developed four new courses specifically designed to bridge gaps between community college prerequisites and Tierra Sandoval’s upper-division computing requirements. These courses were integrated into the existing Computer Information Systems curriculum, providing a smoother academic transition for incoming students.

Furthermore, faculty engagement was prioritized to ensure sustainable support structures. The project facilitated 320 hours of faculty professional development. These sessions focused on pedagogical strategies for diverse learners, advising techniques specific to transfer students, and best practices in computing education. This investment in human capital has resulted in a more responsive academic environment, with faculty better equipped to identify and address the unique needs of transfer scholars.

## Evaluation Findings

The project’s impact was evaluated by Pinyon Ridge Evaluation Partners, led by Dr. Constance Okwuosa. The evaluation confirmed that the direct service model effectively supported student retention and completion. Student feedback indicated high satisfaction with the mentoring components and the clarity of the new course pathways. However, the evaluation also highlighted areas where external partnerships did not align with initial projections, necessitating a transparent discussion of shortfalls in the project’s broader structural goals.

## Shortfalls in Articulation Agreements

While the project achieved its internal academic targets, it fell short of its goal regarding external articulation agreements. The original proposal anticipated signing five new articulation agreements with partner community colleges to formalize credit transfer pathways and streamline admission processes. By the end of the award period, only two of these five planned agreements were successfully signed and implemented.

The failure to secure the remaining three agreements was not due to a lack of effort or interest from Tierra Sandoval’s administration, but rather unforeseen institutional changes at the partner institutions. Specifically, one of the intended partners underwent a significant curriculum revision that rendered their existing computing courses incompatible with the proposed transfer pathways without substantial renegotiation. Another partner institution merged with a neighboring college during the project timeline, causing administrative delays and shifting priorities that stalled negotiations indefinitely. The third anticipated partner withdrew from the process due to internal budget constraints, citing competing institutional needs.

Despite these challenges, the two signed agreements have already begun to yield results, facilitating smoother credit transfers for incoming students from those specific institutions. The lessons learned regarding the volatility of external partnerships will inform our approach to future collaborative efforts.

## Transition to New Initiatives

The insights gained from this project directly inform Tierra Sandoval’s next phase of engagement with NSF funding. As noted in the current cycle, the university is preparing a new proposal under the NSF Scholarships in Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics Program (S-STEM), specifically Solicitation NSF 25-514, Track 2 (Implementation Projects).

The proposed project, titled "Bridging Computing Pathways for Rural Transfer Students" (BCP), will run for six years starting August 1, 2027. This new initiative builds upon the foundation laid by TSU-FICT-HSI-2022, focusing specifically on rural transfer students, a subgroup that faced additional geographic and resource barriers during the prior award. The BCP project aims to support 11 scholars per year, leveraging the curricular innovations and faculty development models tested in the previous grant.

Letters of collaboration have been secured from key regional partners, including Cordillera Basin Community College (dated August 11, 2026) and the Saltbush Regional Workforce Board (dated August 19, 2026), demonstrating continued commitment to expanding access to computing education in our region.

## Conclusion

The "Pathways to Computing Degrees for Transfer Students" project demonstrated that targeted academic support and curricular alignment can significantly improve completion rates for transfer students at Hispanic-Serving Institutions. While the external partnership goals were partially unmet due to institutional instability at partner colleges, the internal outcomes—serving 214 students, achieving 61 completions, developing four new courses, and providing 320 hours of faculty development—validate the project’s core methodology. Tierra Sandoval University remains committed to advancing equity in STEM education, as evidenced by our continued eligibility for HSI designation and our proactive steps toward the upcoming BCP initiative under Dr. President Esperanza Quintanilla-Boyd’s leadership.
