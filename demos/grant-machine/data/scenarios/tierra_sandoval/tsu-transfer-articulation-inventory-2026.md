## Executive Summary and Institutional Context

Tierra Sandoval University, a public regional comprehensive university and designated Hispanic-Serving Institution, continues to prioritize equitable access to higher education within its Inland Southwest service area. With a total enrollment of 9,420 students—of whom 63% are first-generation college attendees and 58% receive Pell Grants—the institution remains deeply committed to closing the attainment gap for underrepresented populations. This commitment is anchored in the leadership of President Dr. Esperanza Quintanilla-Boyd and guided by the strategic objectives outlined in our ongoing grant portfolio.

The foundation for our current initiatives rests upon the successful completion of the prior award, *Pathways to Computing Degrees for Transfer Students* (TSU-FICT-HSI-2022). Funded through the NSF Improving Undergraduate STEM Education: HSI Program, this project operated from September 1, 2022, to August 31, 2025, with a total award amount of $449,800. The prior initiative served 214 students and resulted in 61 transfer completions. Crucially for the current inventory, the prior project facilitated the signing of two formal articulation agreements and established plans for five additional partnerships. This inventory updates the status of those planned agreements as we prepare for the next phase of implementation under the new *Bridging Computing Pathways for Rural Transfer Students* (BCP) project.

### Details of Executed Agreements

The two agreements marked as **Executed** were finalized during the transition period between the prior award and the new BCP solicitation. Both partners have provided formal letters of commitment referencing the BCP project, ensuring continuity of support. Cordillera Basin Community College submitted its letter of support on August 11, 2026, while Saltbush Regional Workforce Board followed on September 19, 2026. These signed agreements validate our capacity to deliver on the transfer completion goals established in the prior award, which previously saw a 28.5% transfer completion rate among served students.

### Status of Agreements in Negotiation

The three remaining partners listed above are currently **in negotiation** and remain unsigned as of today’s date, September 21, 2026. These institutions represent the "planned" agreements identified during the TSU-FICT-HSI-2022 project cycle. The negotiation process involves aligning curriculum mapping between associate-level core courses and Tierra Sandoval’s upper-division requirements, particularly within the Department of Computer Information Systems, chaired by Dr. Elias Vorderman.

The primary objective of these ongoing negotiations is to ensure that students from these rural institutions can seamlessly transition into the BCP track without losing credit hours. The Principal Investigator, Dr. Nayeli Arbuthnot, Associate Provost for Student Success, has designated this alignment as a critical path item for the 2027 launch. While the letters of support from Cordillera Basin and Saltbush confirm institutional buy-in, the formal articulation documents for Desert Mesa Community College, Rio Arriba Technical Institute, and Sierra Vista Valley College are pending final review by legal counsel and academic affairs.

## Strategic Implications for BCP

The expansion from two signed agreements to a network of five partners is essential for the viability of the BCP project (NSF 25-514). The requested budget of $1,753,458 over six years relies heavily on the ability to recruit scholars from these specific rural communities. Without formalized articulation pathways, the risk of student attrition during transfer increases significantly. Therefore, the completion of these unsigned agreements is not merely administrative but a core component of the project’s logic model. The indirect cost rate of 41.5% and fringe benefit rate of 29.6% applied to this budget reflect the institutional commitment to sustaining these collaborative structures over the long term.

As we approach the November 18, 2026, deadline for the S-STEM Track 2 implementation proposal, finalizing these three pending agreements will strengthen our demonstration of institutional readiness and regional partnership depth.
