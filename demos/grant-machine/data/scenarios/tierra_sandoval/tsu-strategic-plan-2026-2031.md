## Executive Summary and Institutional Context

Tierra Sandoval University, a public regional comprehensive university and designated Hispanic-Serving Institution (HSI), serves the Inland Southwest with a commitment to equitable access and rigorous academic preparation. With a total enrollment of 9,420 students, our institution reflects the demographic realities of our service area: 26.4% of our student body identifies as Hispanic, while 58% are Pell Grant recipients and 63% are first-generation college students. This high-need profile underscores our institutional imperative to close completion gaps and align our academic offerings with regional workforce demands.

Under the leadership of President Dr. Esperanza Quintanilla-Boyd, Tierra Sandoval has prioritized the expansion of computing and health sciences programs while strengthening pathways for transfer students. The following strategic plan outlines our approach for 2026–2031, leveraging prior success in STEM education to launch the "Bridging Computing Pathways for Rural Transfer Students" (BCP) project. This initiative responds directly to NSF Solicitation 25-514 under the S-STEM program’s Track 2 (Implementation Projects), aiming to institutionalize sustainable support structures for rural transfer scholars.

## Strategic Priority I: Enhancing Transfer Student Success through Computing Pathways

The core of our strategic vision is the seamless transition of community college students into degree-completing pathways at Tierra Sandoval. Our prior NSF-funded award, *Pathways to Computing Degrees for Transfer Students* (TSU-FICT-HSI-2022), provided a robust foundation for this work. Running from September 1, 2022, through August 31, 2025, that project served 214 students and resulted in 61 transfer completions. During this period, we developed four new courses, facilitated 320 hours of faculty professional development, signed two articulation agreements, and planned five additional agreements.

Building on this legacy, the BCP project will expand these efforts by recruiting and supporting 11 scholars per year over six years, beginning August 1, 2027. The primary goal is to increase transfer completion rates in computing disciplines among rural, first-generation students. By integrating academic advising with financial support and mentorship, we aim to reduce attrition during the critical transition period between community college and university-level coursework.

## Strategic Priority II: Regional Workforce Alignment and Community Engagement

To ensure our graduates are prepared for local employment opportunities, Tierra Sandoval is deepening its partnerships with regional stakeholders. We have secured formal letters of support from Cordillera Basin Community College (dated August 11, 2026) and the Saltbush Regional Workforce Board (dated August 19, 2026). These collaborations are integral to the BCP project’s design.

Cordillera Basin Community College will provide a steady pipeline of qualified transfer applicants, while the Saltbush Regional Workforce Board will ensure that our computing curriculum aligns with current and projected regional labor market needs. This alignment guarantees that our students not only complete their degrees but also secure meaningful employment in high-growth sectors within the Inland Southwest.

## Strategic Priority III: Faculty Development and Program Expansion

Faculty development remains a cornerstone of our institutional strategy. The success of our previous award demonstrated that targeted professional development enhances instructional quality and student outcomes. For the 2026–2031 period, we will continue to invest in faculty capacity building, particularly within the Department of Computer Information Systems, chaired by Dr. Elias Vorderman.

We are also expanding our health sciences programs to complement computing initiatives, creating interdisciplinary opportunities for students. This expansion is supported by the appointment of Dr. Nayeli Arbuthnot as Associate Provost for Student Success, who will oversee the integration of student support services across both colleges. Her leadership ensures that academic excellence and student well-being are pursued in tandem.

## Implementation Plan and Leadership Structure

The BCP project will be administered under the guidance of Marisela Thorncroft, Director of Grants and Contracts, ensuring fiscal compliance and strategic oversight. The project team includes Dr. Nayeli Arbuthnot as Principal Investigator and Dr. Elias Vorderman as Co-Principal Investigator. Their combined expertise in student success metrics and computing curriculum design positions us to achieve the project’s ambitious goals.

## Budget and Resource Allocation

The total requested funding for the BCP project is $1,753,458, distributed evenly at $292,243 per year over six years. This budget accounts for a fringe benefit rate of 29.6% (per agreement tsu-rate-agreement-fy2026) and an indirect cost rate of 41.5% on Modified Total Direct Costs (MTDC). These resources will support scholar stipends, faculty release time, curriculum development, and administrative overhead necessary to sustain the project through its completion.

Tierra Sandoval University is uniquely positioned to address the educational needs of our diverse student population.
