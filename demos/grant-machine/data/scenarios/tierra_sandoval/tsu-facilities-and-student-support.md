## Institutional Context and Capacity

Tierra Sandoval University operates as a public regional comprehensive university located within the Inland Southwest service area. As a designated Hispanic-Serving Institution (HSI), the university is committed to expanding educational access and success for underrepresented student populations. The current enrollment profile reflects this mission, with a total student body of 9,420 individuals. Of this population, 26.4% identify as Hispanic, while significant proportions of students face economic and academic barriers: 58% are recipients of Pell Grants, and 63% are first-generation college students. The university’s HSI designation status is currently eligible, with confirmation secured for the present cycle, underscoring its institutional commitment to serving these demographics.

Under the leadership of President Dr. Esperanza Quintanilla-Boyd, Tierra Sandoval has established a robust infrastructure for student success and research administration. The institution maintains strong administrative capacity through its Office of Grants and Contracts, directed by Marisela Thorncroft, which oversees the rigorous management of federal funding. This administrative framework ensures compliance with federal standards and supports the strategic allocation of resources toward institutional priorities.

## Student Support Services and Institutional Research

Recognizing the diverse needs of its student body, Tierra Sandoval offers comprehensive support services designed to enhance retention and graduation rates. Tutoring services are available across key disciplines, providing targeted academic assistance to students who may be navigating their first year in higher education or struggling with complex technical subjects. These services are integrated with advising programs that guide students through degree planning, career exploration, and personal development.

A critical component of the student support ecosystem is the Transfer Centre. This office facilitates seamless transitions for students moving from community colleges to Tierra Sandoval, ensuring that credits are applied appropriately and that transfer students receive immediate academic integration. The centre works closely with partner institutions to streamline articulation processes, reducing administrative burdens on students and accelerating their progress toward degree completion.

The Institutional Research Office plays a vital role in sustaining these support services by providing data-driven insights into student outcomes and institutional effectiveness. This office collects and analyzes data on enrollment trends, retention rates, and graduation outcomes, informing strategic decisions about resource allocation and program development. The findings from this office have been instrumental in refining the university’s approach to serving first-generation and low-income students.

## Prior Performance and Collaborative Infrastructure

Tierra Sandoval’s capacity to manage complex federal awards is demonstrated by its prior performance record. The institution successfully administered the award *Pathways to Computing Degrees for Transfer Students* (TSU-FICT-HSI-2022), funded by the NSF Improving Undergraduate STEM Education: HSI Program. This project, led by Principal Investigator Dr. Nayeli Arbuthnot, Associate Provost for Student Success, ran from September 1, 2022, to August 31, 2025, with a total budget of $449,800.

The outcomes of this prior award validate the university’s operational capabilities and commitment to STEM education. The project served 214 students and resulted in 61 transfer completions, demonstrating effective pathways for student progression. Additionally, the project contributed to curriculum enhancement by developing four new courses and providing 320 hours of faculty professional development. Furthermore, it strengthened institutional partnerships by signing two articulation agreements, with five additional agreements currently in planning stages. These achievements highlight the university’s ability to deliver tangible educational outcomes and build sustainable collaborative networks.

The current project, *Bridging Computing Pathways for Rural Transfer Students* (BCP), builds upon this foundation. The university has secured letters of support from key collaborators, including Cordillera Basin Community College and the Saltbush Regional Workforce Board, dated August 11 and August 19, 2026, respectively. These partnerships ensure that the new initiative will be grounded in regional workforce needs and community college transfer dynamics. With a six-year implementation period starting August 1, 2027, and a projected cohort of 11 scholars per year, Tierra Sandoval is positioned to leverage its existing facilities, support services, and administrative expertise to achieve significant impacts in computing education for rural transfer students.
