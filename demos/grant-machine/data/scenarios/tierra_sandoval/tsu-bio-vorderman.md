## Biographical Sketch

**Dr. Elias Vorderman** serves as the Chair of the Department of Computer Information Systems at Tierra Sandoval University, a public regional comprehensive university and Hispanic-Serving Institution (HSI) located in the Inland Southwest. With a total enrollment of 9,420 students—of whom 63% are first-generation college students and 58% receive Pell Grants—Dr. Vorderman’s leadership is deeply embedded in the institution’s mission to expand access and success for underrepresented populations. As the designated co-investigator for the proposed project *Bridging Computing Pathways for Rural Transfer Students* (BCP), Dr. Vorderman brings extensive experience in curriculum design, faculty development, and strategic partnership management to this initiative.

### Teaching and Curriculum Development Record

Dr. Vorderman’s academic career has been defined by a commitment to pedagogical innovation and the alignment of computer information systems curricula with workforce needs. In his capacity as department chair, he has overseen the integration of industry-standard technologies into the undergraduate program, ensuring that graduates are prepared for immediate entry into the tech sector. His teaching portfolio includes advanced courses in database management, software engineering, and systems analysis, where he has consistently received high evaluations for clarity and practical application.

A significant component of Dr. Vorderman’s record involves the development of transfer-friendly curricula. He previously served as a key architect for the department’s articulation agreements, which were critical to the success of the prior NSF-funded award, *Pathways to Computing Degrees for Transfer Students* (TSU-FICT-HSI-2022). During that three-year period (September 1, 2022 – August 31, 2025), Dr. Vorderman led a team that developed four new courses specifically designed to bridge gaps between community college coursework and Tierra Sandoval’s upper-division requirements. These efforts contributed to the completion of degrees by 61 transfer students among the 214 served. His focus on curriculum coherence has facilitated the signing of two formal articulation agreements, with five additional agreements currently in planning stages, demonstrating his sustained ability to build structural pathways for student mobility.

### Current and Pending Support

Dr. Vorderman’s current research and administrative portfolio is focused on scaling successful models from previous grants to new geographic and demographic contexts. He serves as Co-Investigator on the proposed project *Bridging Computing Pathways for Rural Transfer Students* (BCP), which seeks support under NSF Solicitation 25-514, the NSF Scholarships in Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics Program (S-STEM), Track 2. This six-year initiative, beginning August 1, 2027, aims to serve 11 scholars annually, building upon the foundational work established by the prior HSI award.

In addition to the proposed BCP grant, Dr. Vorderman remains actively engaged in the administration and evaluation of the prior award (TSU-FICT-HSI-2022), which concludes in August 2025. His role involves overseeing the final reporting phases, including the analysis of outcomes data related to student retention and degree completion. He also collaborates with external partners, including Cordillera Basin Community College and the Saltbush Regional Workforce Board, both of whom have provided letters of support dated August 11 and August 19, 2026, respectively, for the BCP project. These partnerships underscore his ability to mobilize regional stakeholders around shared educational goals.

Dr. Vorderman does not hold any other pending federal or private grants that would conflict with this commitment. His administrative duties as Chair of the Department of Computer Information Systems are fully compatible with the proposed role, allowing him to dedicate substantial effort to the mentoring and academic support structures required for the BCP scholars. Under the leadership of President Dr. Esperanza Quintanilla-Boyd and in coordination with Grants Director Marisela Thorncroft, Dr. Vorderman is positioned to leverage Tierra Sandoval’s HSI designation to maximize the impact of this investment in rural computing education.
