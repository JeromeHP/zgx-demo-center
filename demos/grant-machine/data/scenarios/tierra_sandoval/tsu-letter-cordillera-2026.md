## Letter of Collaboration — Cordillera Basin Community College

**Date:** August 11, 2026

**To:** Dr. Esperanza Quintanilla-Boyd, President, Tierra Sandoval University
**From:** [Name Redacted for Fictional Demo], President, Cordillera Basin Community College
**Re:** Support for the “Bridging Computing Pathways for Rural Transfer Students” (BCP) Project

Dear President Quintanilla-Boyd and Dr. Nayeli Arbuthnot,

Cordillera Basin Community College is pleased to formally confirm our collaboration with Tierra Sandoval University for the proposed National Science Foundation Scholarships in Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (S-STEM) project titled “Bridging Computing Pathways for Rural Transfer Students.” This partnership builds upon a shared commitment to expanding access to computing education for first-generation and low-income students in our region.

### Articulation and Transfer Pathway

Our institutions have established a robust articulation framework that ensures seamless credit transfer for students pursuing degrees in Computer Information Systems. As noted in Tierra Sandoval’s prior NSF award (TSU-FICT-HSI-2022), which successfully signed two articulation agreements, we intend to expand this network under the new BCP initiative. Cordillera Basin will align its Associate of Science in Computer Science curriculum with Tierra Sandoval’s lower-division requirements, ensuring that students completing their associate degrees are fully prepared for junior-level coursework at Tierra Sandoval.

Specifically, Cordillera Basin will guarantee admission to Tierra Sandoval for all BCP scholars who maintain a 2.5 GPA and complete the required preparatory sequence. We will also co-develop four new hybrid courses that can be taken at either institution, providing flexibility for rural students who may face transportation barriers. This approach directly supports the prior project’s outcome of developing new course materials, aiming to increase transfer completion rates beyond the previous cohort of 61 students served.

### Advising and Student Support Coordination

Beyond academic alignment, our collaboration includes coordinated advising services. Cordillera Basin will assign dedicated transfer advisors to each BCP scholar during their first two years, focusing on course selection, financial aid navigation, and early identification of academic challenges. These advisors will maintain bi-monthly contact with Tierra Sandoval’s Student Success team, led by Dr. Nayeli Arbuthnot, to monitor scholar progress and address any barriers to retention.

Tierra Sandoval, in turn, will provide summer bridge programming and mentorship opportunities for incoming BCP scholars. This integrated advising model leverages the strengths of both institutions: Cordillera Basin’s proximity to rural communities and Tierra Sandoval’s resources as a Hispanic-Serving Institution. By combining these efforts, we aim to support 11 scholars per year over six years, with a focus on retaining students in computing pathways who might otherwise disengage due to lack of guidance or financial strain.

### Commitment and Resources

Cordillera Basin Community College commits to providing necessary faculty time for curriculum alignment, advising coordination, and joint evaluation activities. We view this collaboration as critical to addressing the regional workforce gap in computing and ensuring that our rural students have clear, supported pathways to four-year degrees. We look forward to working closely with Dr. Elias Vorderman, Chair of Computer Information Systems at Tierra Sandoval, and Marisela Thorncroft, Director of Grants and Contracts, to ensure the successful implementation of this project.
