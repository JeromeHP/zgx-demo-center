## Letter of Collaboration — Saltbush Regional Workforce Board

**To:** The Selection Committee, NSF S-STEM Program (Solicitation 25-514)
**From:** Saltbush Regional Workforce Board
**Re:** Support for *Bridging Computing Pathways for Rural Transfer Students* (BCP)
**Date:** August 19, 2026

The Saltbush Regional Workforce Board hereby confirms its enthusiastic collaboration with Tierra Sandoval University in support of the proposed project, *Bridging Computing Pathways for Rural Transfer Students* (BCP). As a key partner in the Inland Southwest’s economic development ecosystem, our organization recognizes that sustainable regional growth depends on a robust, skilled technology workforce. We have reviewed the BCP proposal and affirm that its objectives align seamlessly with our strategic priority to increase local employment retention in high-demand STEM sectors.

### Employer Connections and Industry Alignment

Our board maintains active partnerships with over forty-five regional employers, including major tech hubs and rural manufacturing firms currently undergoing digital transformation. Through these networks, we have identified a critical shortage of entry-level computing professionals among transfer students who often lack the localized professional capital required to secure employment immediately upon graduation. The BCP project’s focus on providing targeted internships directly addresses this gap. We commit to facilitating introductions between Tierra Sandoval scholars and our member employers, ensuring that internship placements are not merely administrative formalities but meaningful, skill-building experiences aligned with current industry standards.

### Internship Placement Support

The Saltbush Regional Workforce Board will provide dedicated staff support to streamline the internship placement process for the BCP cohort. Specifically, we will:

1.  **Curate Employer Opportunities:** Work with Tierra Sandoval’s Department of Computer Information Systems to identify and vet internship positions that offer mentorship and technical training, ensuring they meet the pedagogical goals of the program.
2.  **Facilitate Matchmaking:** Utilize our regional employer database to match BCP scholars with roles that complement their academic trajectories, particularly focusing on firms willing to hire rural transfer students who may face geographic barriers to traditional tech hubs.
3.  **Provide Career Readiness Resources:** Offer pre-internship workshops on professional communication, workplace ethics, and technical interview preparation, leveraging our existing workforce development infrastructure to enhance scholar employability.

### Commitment to Long-Term Impact

This collaboration is designed to create a sustainable pipeline from academia to industry. By integrating Tierra Sandoval’s academic rigor with our regional employer network, we aim to reduce the time-to-hire for participating scholars and increase the long-term retention of computing talent within the Inland Southwest. We anticipate that this partnership will not only benefit the 11 scholars per year served by the BCP project but also serve as a model for other institutions seeking to bridge the gap between higher education and regional workforce needs.

We look forward to working closely with Dr. Nayeli Arbuthnot, Dr. Elias Vorderman, and the Tierra Sandoval grants team to ensure the successful implementation of this initiative.

Sincerely,

Executive Director
