## Executive Summary and Strategic Context

The Sablecreek State University System (Sablecreek System), under the leadership of Chancellor Dr. Ingrid Velásquez-Moor, is positioned to leverage its unique geographic footprint across the Northern plains to address critical regional challenges. With a system enrollment of 47,500 and an annual research expenditure of $96,000,000, the System possesses the scale and capacity to drive transformative research. This document outlines the System Research Strategy for 2026-2031, identifying four priority areas: precision agriculture and working lands, rural health access, energy systems, and shared research infrastructure.

The strategy builds upon the success of prior initiatives, specifically the Statewide Research Infrastructure for Precision Agriculture (SSUS-FICT-EPSCOR-2021), funded by the NSF EPSCoR program. That award, which concluded on August 31, 2026, successfully engaged 47 faculty members across three campuses, deployed six shared instruments, and supported 22 graduate students. Building on this foundation, the System proposes a new collaborative infrastructure project to extend these capabilities to all four member campuses and deepen industry partnerships.

## Research Priority I: Precision Agriculture and Working Lands

Precision agriculture remains the cornerstone of the Sablecreek System’s research portfolio, given the region’s economic dependence on agricultural productivity. The transition from the prior EPSCoR award to a more robust, system-wide infrastructure is critical for maintaining competitive grant acquisition in this domain.

**Lead Institution:** Sablecreek State Polytechnic — Grier Falls will serve as the primary lead campus for precision agriculture initiatives, leveraging its technical expertise and existing facilities. Dr. Marlowe Sandoval-Teague, Campus Research Lead at Grier Falls, has been designated as Co-Principal Investigator to oversee the technical integration of new sensing technologies.

**Strategic Approach:** The System proposes the "Shared Sensing Infrastructure for Working Lands Across the System" (SSI). This project aims to deploy a unified network of environmental and crop-monitoring sensors across the Northern plains. By consolidating data collection efforts, the System will reduce redundancy and enhance the resolution of agricultural data available to local farmers and policymakers. The SSI project is aligned with NSF Solicitation 25-523 (E-CORE) and has a committed cost share of $250,000. Dr. Augustin Rieckhoff, System Vice Chancellor for Research, serves as Principal Investigator, ensuring alignment with system-wide goals.

## Research Priority II: Rural Health Access

The second priority area addresses the persistent disparities in healthcare access across the Northern plains. The Sablecreek System will utilize its distributed campus network to develop telehealth solutions and mobile health clinics tailored to remote communities.

**Lead Institution:** Sablecreek Community and Technical College — Ondrak will lead efforts in rural health access, focusing on workforce development and community-based health interventions. Ondrak’s proximity to underserved populations allows for direct engagement with community stakeholders, ensuring that research outcomes are practically applicable.

**Strategic Approach:** Research in this area will focus on three pillars: telehealth infrastructure deployment, training of rural health professionals, and evaluation of health outcomes in remote settings. Collaboration between the medical programs at Harlow Bend and the technical support capabilities at Ondrak will create a comprehensive model for rural health delivery. The System aims to leverage its $96 million annual research budget to support pilot studies that can later be scaled through federal health grants.

## Research Priority III: Energy Systems

Energy security and sustainability are critical for the Northern plains, particularly in the context of renewable energy integration and grid resilience. The Sablecreek System will prioritize research into smart grid technologies and renewable energy storage solutions.

**Lead Institution:** Sablecreek State University — Harlow Bend will lead the energy systems research cluster. Harlow Bend’s established engineering programs and existing partnerships with regional energy providers provide a strong foundation for this initiative. A letter of support from Harlow Bend, dated August 7, 2026, confirms its commitment to this strategic direction.

**Strategic Approach:** The energy systems priority will focus on developing resilient microgrid architectures suitable for rural and semi-rural applications. Research teams will investigate the integration of solar and wind resources with existing grid infrastructure, addressing challenges related to intermittency and storage. This work will be supported by shared computational resources and collaboration with industry partners in the region.

## Research Priority IV: Shared Research Infrastructure

To sustain excellence across all priority areas, the Sablecreek System must invest in a robust, shared research infrastructure. This includes high-performance computing clusters, advanced laboratory facilities, and data management platforms that are accessible to all four campuses.

**Lead Institution:** The entire System will share responsibility for infrastructure development, with administrative oversight provided by the System Office. Delphine Okonkwo-Barr, System Director of Sponsored Programs, will manage the financial and compliance aspects of infrastructure investments.

**Strategic Approach:** The SSI project serves as the initial step in this broader infrastructure strategy. By establishing a shared sensing network for agriculture, the System creates a template for data sharing and collaborative research that can be adapted for health and energy domains. The indirect cost rate agreement (47.0% on MTDC) and fringe benefit rate (30.4%) ensure that infrastructure costs are adequately covered. The System will continue to seek external funding to expand these shared resources, aiming to increase the number of participating campuses from three to four in all major research clusters.
