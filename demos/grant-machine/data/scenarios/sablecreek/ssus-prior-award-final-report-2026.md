## Executive Summary and Institutional Context

This document serves as the comprehensive final report for the prior EPSCoR award, *Statewide Research Infrastructure for Precision Agriculture* (Award ID: SSUS-FICT-EPSCOR-2021), funded by the National Science Foundation. Conducted under the auspices of the Sablecreek State University System (Sablecreek System), this report details the execution, outcomes, and financial stewardship of the project from its inception on September 1, 2021, through its conclusion on August 31, 2026. The Sablecreek System, a public university system office serving the northern plains with four distinct campuses and an annual research expenditure of $96 million, utilized this award to strengthen regional capacity in agricultural technology.

The project was led by System Vice Chancellor for Research Dr. Augustin Rieckhoff, with Dr. Marlowe Sandoval-Teague serving as Campus Research Lead at Grier Falls. Oversight of sponsored programs and financial compliance was managed by System Director of Sponsored Programs Delphine Okonkwo-Barr. Independent evaluation was conducted by Ridgeline Systems Evaluation, led by Dr. Theodora Vasquez-Lund.

## Project Outcomes and Performance Metrics

The primary objective of the award was to establish a cohesive, statewide infrastructure for precision agriculture research. The project successfully engaged 47 faculty members across the system and supported 22 graduate students through stipends and tuition waivers. A total of six shared instruments were deployed across participating campuses, facilitating high-throughput data collection and analysis capabilities that were previously unavailable to individual campus departments.

Furthermore, the infrastructure built during this period directly contributed to the acquisition of five follow-on external awards, demonstrating the project’s success in leveraging initial federal investment into sustained external funding. These outcomes highlight the effective utilization of the $3.2 million award to build critical mass in precision agriculture research within the northern plains region.

## Participation Analysis and Institutional Challenges

While the project achieved significant quantitative success, it is essential to report honestly on the scope of participation relative to the original proposal. The initial plan anticipated engagement from all four member campuses: Sablecreek State University — Harlow Bend; Sablecreek State University — Pellamy; Sablecreek State Polytechnic — Grier Falls; and Sablecreek Community and Technical College — Ondrak.

Final participation records confirm that three of the four planned campuses actively participated in the project: Harlow Bend, Grier Falls, and Ondrak. The fourth campus, Sablecreek State University — Pellamy, did not participate in the execution of this award. This shortfall was not due to a lack of scientific interest but rather stemmed from two compounding administrative issues specific to that institution during the project’s early implementation phase.

First, Sablecreek State University — Pellamy underwent a significant leadership transition shortly after the award period began. The interim administration required additional time to align internal strategic priorities with the system-wide goals of the EPSCoR initiative, resulting in a delay in resource allocation that ultimately precluded participation within the project timeline.

Second, and more critically, an unresolved dispute regarding indirect cost distribution models prevented Pellamy from formally joining the consortium. The campus administration raised concerns about how indirect costs would be calculated and distributed among the system office and participating campuses under the specific terms of this award. Despite multiple mediation efforts led by the System Chancellor, Dr. Ingrid Velásquez-Moor, and the Director of Sponsored Programs, Delphine Okonkwo-Barr, a consensus on the cost-sharing mechanism for indirect expenses could not be reached before the fiscal year cutoff. Consequently, Pellamy was unable to commit the necessary administrative infrastructure to support the grant, leading to their exclusion from the final participant roster.

## Financial Stewardship and Compliance

The Sablecreek System adhered strictly to federal guidelines and institutional policies throughout the life of the award. The total budget of $3,200,000 was managed in accordance with the approved budget narrative. Fringe benefits were calculated at a rate of 30.4%, as defined in the system’s fringe rate agreement (ssus-rate-agreement-fy2026). Indirect costs were applied at a rate of 47.0% on Modified Total Direct Costs (MTDC), consistent with the negotiated indirect cost rate for the fiscal year 2026.

All expenditures were monitored by the System Director of Sponsored Programs to ensure alignment with the allowable cost principles. The financial data, including detailed line-item expenditures and personnel effort reports, is documented in the official outcomes data file (ssus-prior-outcomes-data). No unallowable costs were identified during the independent audit conducted by Ridgeline Systems Evaluation.

## Legacy and Future Directions

The infrastructure established under this award has laid the groundwork for the next phase of collaborative research. The six shared instruments deployed remain operational and are now integrated into the system’s broader sensing network. The 47 faculty members engaged have formed a durable interdisciplinary network that continues to collaborate on proposals beyond the scope of this award.

Building upon these successes, the Sablecreek System is currently preparing a new submission for NSF Solicitation 25-523, titled *Shared Sensing Infrastructure for Working Lands Across the System* (SSI). This new project, classified under Track 2 (E-CORE), seeks to expand the capabilities established in the prior award. The SSI proposal requests $9,644,873 over four years, beginning September 1, 2027. It includes a committed cost share of $250,000 from the institution and leverages the same fringe rate (30.4%) and indirect rate (47.0% on MTDC).

Support for this new initiative is robust. Letters of support have been secured from Harlow Bend (dated August 7, 2026), Grier Falls (dated August 15, 2026), and the Northern Plains Agricultural Commission (dated August 26, 2026). These documents affirm the continued commitment of the system’s leadership and external partners to advancing precision agriculture research.

In conclusion, while the prior award did not achieve full geographic participation across all four campuses due to specific administrative challenges at Sablecreek State University — Pellamy, it succeeded in its core mission of building capacity, supporting talent, and fostering collaboration. The outcomes reported herein reflect a rigorous and productive five-year period that has positioned the Sablecreek System for continued excellence in federally funded research.
