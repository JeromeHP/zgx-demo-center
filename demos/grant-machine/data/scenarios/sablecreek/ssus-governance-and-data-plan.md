## Consortium Governance Structure

The Sablecreek State University System (Sablecreek System) is a public university system office overseeing four campuses across the northern plains: Sablecreek State University — Harlow Bend, Sablecreek State University — Pellamy, Sablecreek State Polytechnic — Grier Falls, and Sablecreek Community and Technical College — Ondrak. With a system-wide enrollment of 47,500 and an annual research expenditure of $96,000,000, the System provides the administrative backbone for this multi-campus initiative. Governance for the Shared Sensing Infrastructure for Working Lands Across the System (SSI) is established through a tiered executive committee structure designed to balance system-wide strategic oversight with campus-level operational autonomy.

The primary governing body is the SSI Executive Committee, chaired by Dr. Ingrid Velásquez-Moor, Chancellor of the Sablecreek System. This committee includes the Principal Investigator, Dr. Augustin Rieckhoff, who serves as System Vice Chancellor for Research, and the Co-Principal Investigator, Dr. Marlowe Sandoval-Teague, Campus Research Lead at Grier Falls. The Committee also comprises senior research administrators from each of the four member campuses and representatives from key external partners, including the Northern Plains Agricultural Commission, whose support letter dated August 26, 2026, underscores the project’s regional importance. The Executive Committee meets quarterly to review progress against milestones, approve budget reallocations exceeding $50,000, and assess strategic alignment with the broader goals of the NSF EPSCoR Collaborations for Optimizing Research Ecosystems (E-CORE) program.

Decision rights are clearly delineated between the System Office and individual campuses. The System Office, through the Director of Sponsored Programs, Delphine Okonkwo-Barr, retains authority over fiscal compliance, indirect cost distribution, and overall project reporting to the National Science Foundation (NSF). Individual campuses retain operational control over personnel hiring, local procurement, and day-to-day management of their respective contributions to the shared sensing infrastructure. This structure leverages the previous successful governance model established under the prior award, SSUS-FICT-EPSCOR-2021, titled "Statewide Research Infrastructure for Precision Agriculture." That initiative, funded by NSF EPSCoR Research Infrastructure Improvement from September 1, 2021, to August 31, 2026, successfully engaged 47 faculty members and supported 22 graduate students across three initial campuses. The SSI project expands this framework to all four campuses, aiming to deploy six shared instruments and secure five external follow-on awards, building on the proven efficacy of the prior administrative model.

Conflict resolution is managed through a formal escalation protocol. Disputes regarding resource allocation or intellectual property are first addressed by the Co-Principal Investigator and Campus Research Leads. If unresolved, they are elevated to the Executive Committee for mediation. Persistent conflicts are referred to the Chancellor’s Office for final arbitration. This process ensures minimal disruption to research activities while maintaining accountability across the diverse institutional cultures of Harlow Bend, Pellamy, Grier Falls, and Ondrak.

## Data Management and Shared Repository Standards

Data generated under the SSI project will be managed through a unified, system-wide data governance framework designed to ensure interoperability, accessibility, and long-term preservation. The primary objective is to create a seamless flow of sensing data from working lands across the northern plains, requiring strict adherence to metadata standards and access protocols.

All research data will be stored in a shared institutional repository hosted centrally by the System Office but accessible via role-based permissions to all four campuses. This architecture prevents data silos and ensures that Harlow Bend, Pellamy, Grier Falls, and Ondrak can contribute to and retrieve from a common pool of information. The repository will utilize standardized metadata schemas aligned with NSF’s data management requirements, ensuring that datasets are discoverable and reusable by the broader scientific community. Metadata standards will be enforced through automated validation tools integrated into the campus research portals, reducing administrative burden on individual investigators while maintaining data integrity.

Access to the shared repository is governed by a tiered permission model. Principal Investigators at each campus hold full read-write access to their respective data streams. Cross-campus data sharing is facilitated through a "need-to-know" protocol, where researchers must request access from the Data Stewardship Committee, composed of representatives from each campus’s research office. This committee, supported by the System Director of Sponsored Programs, reviews access requests to ensure compliance with data use agreements and confidentiality requirements.

The project builds upon the technical infrastructure established during the prior award period (2021–2026). That initiative deployed six shared instruments and generated substantial datasets that informed the current SSI proposal. The new repository will incorporate lessons learned from that experience, particularly regarding the challenges of integrating heterogeneous data formats from agricultural sensors. To support this effort, the project includes provisions for training workshops on metadata standardization and data curation, targeting the 47 faculty members previously engaged in precision agriculture research, with an expanded focus on the additional faculty and graduate students at Ondrak and Pellamy.

Financially, the SSI project requests a total of $9,644,873 over four years, beginning September 1, 2027. This budget includes a committed cost share of $250,000 and utilizes a fringe rate of 30.4% per the SSUS Rate Agreement FY2026 and an indirect cost rate of 47.0% on Modified Total Direct Costs (MTDC). The governance and data management structures described herein are designed to ensure that these resources are utilized efficiently, transparently, and in full compliance with NSF solicitation NSF 25-523 requirements.
