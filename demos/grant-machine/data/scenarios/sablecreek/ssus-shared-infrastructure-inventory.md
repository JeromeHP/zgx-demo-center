## Shared Research Infrastructure Inventory

The Sablecreek State University System operates a robust, interconnected network of research facilities designed to support the unique agricultural and technical needs of the Northern plains region. With an annual research expenditure of $96,000,000 and a total system enrollment of 47,500 students, the institution maintains four distinct campuses that serve as hubs for scientific inquiry and technological development. This inventory outlines the current state of shared instrumentation and facilities, detailing their distribution, access protocols, and operational frameworks across the system.

### Campus Distribution and Facility Overview

The physical infrastructure is distributed across the four member institutions: Sablecreek State University — Harlow Bend, Sablecreek State University — Pellamy, Sablecreek State Polytechnic — Grier Falls, and Sablecreek Community and Technical College — Ondrak. The system’s previous initiative, *Statewide Research Infrastructure for Precision Agriculture* (SSUS-FICT-EPSCOR-2021), successfully deployed six shared instruments across three of these campuses between 2021 and 2026. These prior deployments engaged 47 faculty members and supported 22 graduate students, laying the groundwork for the expanded access proposed under the new *Shared Sensing Infrastructure for Working Lands Across the System* (SSI) project.

Currently, high-capability sensing equipment is primarily concentrated at Sablecreek State University — Harlow Bend and Sablecreek State Polytechnic — Grier Falls. Harlow Bend hosts the central analytical core, while Grier Falls specializes in field-deployable sensor arrays. Pellamy provides supporting computational resources, and Ondrak offers specialized training facilities for undergraduate and technical staff. While all four campuses contribute to the system’s research ecosystem, access to specific high-value instrumentation is not uniformly distributed due to physical location and capacity constraints.

### Access Protocols and Limitations

Access to shared instruments is governed by a tiered protocol designed to prioritize system-wide equity while maintaining operational efficiency. All faculty and graduate students within the Sablecreek System are eligible to request time on shared facilities. However, access to certain specialized instruments is limited by physical capacity and technical complexity. For instance, the high-resolution spectrometry units at Harlow Bend are restricted to scheduled blocks to prevent scheduling conflicts, whereas field sensors deployed across Grier Falls’ agricultural plots operate on a continuous monitoring basis accessible via remote data portals.

External users from non-member institutions may access facilities only if they do not displace internal Sablecreek System users and if the usage aligns with the facility’s primary mission of supporting working lands research. Requests from external partners, such as the Northern Plains Agricultural Commission, are evaluated on a case-by-case basis. The Commission has already demonstrated its commitment to this infrastructure through a formal letter of support dated August 26, 2026, referencing the SSI project.

### Scheduling and Recharging Mechanisms

Usage of shared instrumentation is managed through a centralized digital scheduling platform overseen by the System Director of Sponsored Programs, Delphine Okonkwo-Barr. This platform allows users to view real-time availability, book time slots, and upload preliminary data for quality assurance checks before full access is granted. The system employs a "recharging" model to ensure financial sustainability and fair usage distribution.

Each campus operates under a internal cost-recovery framework where units of usage are charged against departmental research budgets. The fringe rate agreement for the current fiscal year (2026) stands at 30.4%, as documented in *ssus-rate-agreement-fy2026*, and indirect costs are calculated at 47.0% on modified total direct costs (MTDC). These rates are applied to all external and inter-campus transactions to ensure that the maintenance, calibration, and personnel costs associated with instrument uptime are adequately covered.

Under the proposed SSI project, which spans four years from September 1, 2027, to August 31, 2031, the recharging structure will be expanded to include new sensor networks. The system has committed a cost share of $250,000 to support this expansion, ensuring that campus-level contributions match the federal investment. The budget summary for the first year requests $2,808,568, with subsequent years requesting $2,397,938, $2,199,997, and $2,238,370 respectively, totaling $9,644,873 over the project period.
