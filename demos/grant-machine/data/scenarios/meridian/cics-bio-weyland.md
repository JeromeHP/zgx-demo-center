## Position and Training

Dr. Toma Weyland serves as a Research Scientist specializing in Sediment Dynamics at the Calderwick Institute for Coastal Systems (CICS). CICS is a research institute within Alderbrook State University, dedicated to the study of Mid-Atlantic estuarine and nearshore systems. As part of this public doctoral university, the institute employs 41 research staff and supports 28 graduate students, operating with an annual research expenditure of $9.4 million under the leadership of Director Dr. Halvard Mensing.

Dr. Weyland’s academic foundation is rooted in physical oceanography and geomorphology, with specialized training in high-resolution numerical modeling and field-based sediment transport analysis. His technical expertise bridges the gap between theoretical hydrodynamics and practical coastal management, allowing him to effectively translate complex data into actionable insights for vulnerable communities.

## Research Focus

Dr. Weyland’s research focuses on the intricate mechanics of sediment dynamics within microtidal estuaries, particularly how these systems respond to compound flooding events. His work investigates the coupling between atmospheric forcing and nearshore hydrodynamics, aiming to improve predictive capabilities for under-instrumented regions. This focus is critical for enhancing resilience in coastal zones where data scarcity often hinders accurate risk assessment.

Currently, Dr. Weyland is a Co-Investigator on the new project "Compound Flood Forecasting for Under-Instrumented Estuaries" (CFFUE). This three-year initiative, commencing July 1, 2027, seeks to develop advanced forecasting models that integrate sediment transport data with flood prediction algorithms. The project falls under Track 2 of the NSF Solicitation 26-516, within the Ocean Sciences program of the Geosciences Core Research (GEO Core) division.
