## Executive Summary and Strategic Context

The Calderwick Institute for Coastal Systems (CICS), a research institute within Alderbrook State University, is submitting this proposal to address critical gaps in coastal resilience planning across the Mid-Atlantic estuarine and nearshore systems. Under the leadership of Director Dr. Halvard Mensing, CICS leverages its team of 41 research staff and 28 graduate students to tackle complex environmental challenges with an annual research expenditure of $9,400,000. This document outlines our five-year Research Strategic Priorities (2026-2031), focusing on five interconnected pillars: compound flood forecasting, sediment transport under changing storm regimes, estuarine ecosystem response, data infrastructure for under-instrumented coastlines, and workforce development for coastal resilience practitioners.

Our strategic vision is grounded in the urgent need to protect vulnerable communities from escalating climate-driven hazards. By integrating advanced modeling techniques with robust field data collection, CICS aims to enhance predictive capabilities and inform policy decisions that safeguard ecological integrity and human safety. This proposal seeks support through NSF Solicitation 26-516 under the Geosciences Core Research program to advance these priorities, specifically through our new project, Compound Flood Forecasting for Under-Instrumented Estuaries (CFFUE).

## Prior Achievements and Institutional Capacity

CICS has established a strong track record of delivering high-impact research outcomes. A key precedent is our prior award, CICS-FICT-OCE-2021, titled "Sediment Transport Under Compound Flooding in Microtidal Estuaries," funded by the NSF Division of Ocean Sciences from August 1, 2021, to July 31, 2025. This project, with an award amount of $842,600, successfully supported four graduate students and resulted in nine peer-reviewed publications and three published datasets. The team completed five field campaigns, planned eight additional campaigns, and deployed 22 instruments, demonstrating our capacity to execute complex, multi-year research initiatives.

Building on this foundation, CICS is poised to expand its impact through the proposed CFFUE project. With a total requested budget of $1,029,198 over three years (starting July 1, 2027), we will address critical data gaps in under-instrumented estuaries. The budget breakdown includes $384,633 for Year 1, $318,594 for Year 2, and $325,971 for Year 3. Applying our approved fringe rate of 31.5% (per agreement cics-rate-agreement-fy2026) and an indirect cost rate of 54.5% on Modified Total Direct Costs (MTDC), we ensure fiscal responsibility and compliance with federal guidelines.

## Strategic Priority I: Compound Flood Forecasting for Under-Instrumented Estuaries

The first strategic priority focuses on developing advanced forecasting models for compound flooding events in regions lacking dense observational networks. CFFUE, led by Associate Director Dr. Imogen Sarkissian and Research Scientist Dr. Toma Weyland, will integrate atmospheric, oceanic, and hydrological data to improve prediction accuracy. This project directly responds to Solicitation 26-516’s emphasis on Ocean Sciences and aims to bridge the gap between theoretical models and practical forecasting tools.

Collaborative partnerships are central to this initiative. We have secured letters of support from Rowan Tidal Observatory (dated August 5, 2026) and Quillon County Emergency Management (dated August 18, 2026), both referencing the CFFUE project. These collaborations ensure access to critical tidal data and facilitate the translation of research findings into actionable emergency management strategies. By leveraging these networks, CFFUE will enhance community preparedness and reduce vulnerability to compound flood events.

## Strategic Priority II: Sediment Transport Under Changing Storm Regimes

The second priority examines how shifting storm patterns alter sediment dynamics in estuarine environments. Dr. Toma Weyland’s expertise in sediment dynamics will guide this effort, building upon the insights gained from the prior CICS-FICT-OCE-2021 award. We aim to quantify the long-term impacts of increased storm frequency and intensity on coastal morphology and ecosystem health. This research will inform adaptive management strategies for coastal infrastructure and habitat restoration.

## Strategic Priority III: Estuarine Ecosystem Response

Understanding the ecological consequences of physical changes is vital for holistic coastal management. CICS will investigate how alterations in hydrodynamics and sedimentation affect biodiversity, water quality, and habitat structure in Mid-Atlantic estuaries. By linking physical processes to biological responses, we aim to develop indicators of ecosystem resilience that can guide conservation efforts and restore ecological balance.

## Strategic Priority IV: Data Infrastructure for Under-Instrumented Coastlines

A significant barrier to effective coastal management is the scarcity of high-quality data in remote or underserved areas. CICS will invest in deploying low-cost, durable sensor networks to fill these data gaps. This infrastructure will support real-time monitoring and long-term trend analysis, enabling more accurate modeling and forecasting. The data collected will be openly shared with the scientific community and stakeholders, fostering collaboration and innovation.

## Strategic Priority V: Workforce Development for Coastal Resilience Practitioners

Finally, CICS is committed to cultivating the next generation of coastal resilience professionals. Through graduate student support, internships, and professional development workshops, we will equip practitioners with the skills needed to address complex coastal challenges. This initiative ensures that our research translates into tangible benefits for communities and that our workforce is prepared to lead in a rapidly changing environment.
