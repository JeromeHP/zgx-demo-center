## Facilities and Core Infrastructure

The Calderwick Institute for Coastal Systems (CICS), located within Alderbrook State University, operates as a specialized research entity dedicated to mid-Atlantic estuarine and nearshore systems. Our institutional infrastructure is designed to support high-resolution hydrodynamic modeling, sediment transport analysis, and real-time environmental monitoring. The physical and computational resources available to the *Compound Flood Forecasting for Under-Instrumented Estuaries* (CFFUE) project are categorized into those directly controlled by CICS and those accessed through shared university or collaborative networks.

### Directly Controlled Research Facilities

CICS maintains a dedicated coastal sediment flume facility, which serves as the primary laboratory for validating numerical models under controlled conditions. This facility allows researchers to simulate compound flooding scenarios with precise control over tidal forcing, wave action, and sediment grain size distributions. The flume is equipped with high-speed particle image velocimetry (PIV) systems and laser-induced fluorescence (LIF) units, enabling detailed quantification of turbulent kinetic energy and mixing processes at the air-water interface.

In addition to laboratory capabilities, CICS directly manages a robust inventory of moored field instruments. This inventory includes multiple Acoustic Doppler Current Profilers (ADCPs), pressure sensors for tide gauge validation, and optical turbidity loggers calibrated for high-salinity estuarine environments. These assets are critical for the CFFUE project’s objective of deploying low-cost sensor arrays in under-instrumented regions. The institute also operates a small research vessel, *RV Calderwick*, which is exclusively reserved for institute use. This vessel supports short-duration field campaigns, instrument recovery, and the deployment of temporary moorings in shallow estuarine waters where larger university-owned ships cannot operate safely.

### Shared University Resources

While CICS controls its specialized physical assets, computational and storage resources are leveraged through the parent institution, Alderbrook State University. The institute utilizes shared high-performance computing (HPC) clusters provided by the university’s Center for Scientific Computing. Access to these supercomputing resources is granted via a dedicated institutional account managed by CICS research administration. This access enables the execution of large-scale hydrodynamic simulations required for forecasting compound flood events, particularly those involving complex interactions between atmospheric pressure gradients and tidal harmonics.

Data storage for the CFFUE project is similarly shared. Primary raw data from field deployments are ingested into Alderbrook State University’s centralized research data repository, which offers petabyte-scale storage capacity and redundant backup systems. However, CICS retains direct administrative control over the curation and metadata standards applied to these datasets, ensuring they meet NSF FAIR principles. Secondary processed data and model outputs are archived in a separate, institute-managed cloud storage bucket, providing faster access for real-time forecasting applications while maintaining compliance with university data governance policies.

## Equipment Availability and Collaborative Assets

The success of the CFFUE project relies on the seamless integration of CICS’s proprietary equipment with external collaborative networks. The institute’s direct control over its ADCP and turbidity logger inventory ensures that deployment schedules are not bottlenecked by competing departmental needs. These instruments are routinely maintained and calibrated within CICS workshops, reducing downtime during field campaigns.

Furthermore, CICS has established formal collaborations that extend our resource base without requiring direct ownership of the assets.
