## Institutional Overview and Administrative Structure

The Calderwick Institute for Coastal Systems (CICS) operates as a specialized research institute within Alderbrook State University, a public doctoral institution. Established to address complex challenges in coastal science, CICS serves the Mid-Atlantic estuarine and nearshore systems, focusing on the intersection of physical oceanography, sediment transport mechanics, and ecological resilience. The institute is governed by a clear administrative hierarchy designed to support rigorous scientific inquiry while maintaining strict fiscal compliance with federal funding standards.

Dr. Halvard Mensing serves as the Director of CICS, providing overarching strategic leadership for the institute’s research portfolio. Day-to-day operational management and grant administration are overseen by Beatrix Okonjo-Hale, who holds the title of Director of Research Administration. Ms. Okonjo-Hale ensures that all institutional expenditures align with Alderbrook State University’s indirect cost recovery policies and fringe benefit agreements. The institute currently operates under a negotiated indirect cost rate of 54.5% applied to Modified Total Direct Costs (MTDC), with a fringe benefit rate agreement of 31.5%, as documented in the FY2026 institutional rate agreement (cics-rate-agreement-fy2026).

## Research Capacity and Personnel

CICS maintains a compact, highly specialized workforce dedicated to high-impact coastal research. The institute employs 41 research staff members, including principal investigators, postdoctoral fellows, and technical specialists. These professionals are supported by a cohort of 28 graduate students who contribute to field campaigns, data analysis, and model development. This staffing structure allows CICS to maintain a lean operational footprint while delivering significant scientific output relative to its size.

The institute’s research is organized into three primary groups: Coastal Process Modelling, Sediment Dynamics, and Estuarine Ecology. Dr. Imogen Sarkissian serves as the Associate Director for Coastal Process Modelling, bringing extensive expertise in hydrodynamic simulation and numerical modeling techniques. In the Sediment Dynamics group, Dr. Toma Weyland leads research efforts as a Research Scientist, focusing on granular transport mechanisms and morphological change. The Estuarine Ecology group complements these physical sciences by investigating biological responses to sediment load variations and water quality shifts.
