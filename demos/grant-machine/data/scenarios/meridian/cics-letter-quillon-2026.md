## Operational Integration of Forecast Products

The Quillon County Emergency Management Office (QCEM) confirms its intent to integrate forecast products generated by the Compound Flood Forecasting for Under-Instrumented Estuaries (CFFUE) project into our daily operational workflows. As a jurisdiction characterized by complex microtidal estuarine systems, QCEM faces significant challenges in predicting compound flooding events where storm surge and fluvial discharge interact. The CFFUE model outputs will serve as a critical decision-support tool for our emergency operations center, particularly during the early stages of tropical cyclone development.

Our operational team will utilize the high-resolution nearshore hydrodynamic forecasts to validate evacuation zone boundaries in real-time. Specifically, we will cross-reference CICS’s predicted inundation depths with our existing coastal flood maps to adjust shelter capacity and resource allocation. This integration allows us to move from static, pre-disaster planning to dynamic, event-specific response strategies. The forecast products will be ingested directly into our Geographic Information Systems (GIS) platform, enabling seamless visualization for both internal staff and public-facing emergency alerts.

## Evaluation of Usability and Impact

QCEM recognizes that the technical accuracy of a model is insufficient without practical usability. Therefore, we have established a formal evaluation framework to assess the effectiveness of these forecast products throughout the three-year duration of the CFFUE project. Our role in this evaluation focuses on two primary metrics: timeliness and interpretability.

First, we will document the latency between data release and operational deployment. We aim to determine if the forecast lead times are sufficient for our current evacuation protocols, which typically require 12–24 hours of preparation. Second, we will evaluate the clarity of the data visualization. Our incident commanders must be able to interpret complex sediment transport and water level data under high-stress conditions. To facilitate this, QCEM will assign two senior planners to provide weekly feedback on the interface design and data presentation formats.

We will conduct structured debriefings after each major weather event to assess how well the CFFUE forecasts aligned with observed realities. These debriefs will inform the iterative refinement of the model by Dr. Imogen Sarkissian’s team at the Calderwick Institute for Coastal Systems (CICS). By providing this operational feedback, we ensure that the research outcomes directly address the practical needs of first responders and community planners.

## Commitment to Collaboration

QCEM is committed to supporting the dissemination of CFFUE results through case studies derived from our emergency responses. We agree to share anonymized operational logs and post-event assessments with Dr. Toma Weyland and the broader research team. This collaboration exemplifies the synergy between academic research and public safety practice, ensuring that the $1,029,198 investment in CFFUE yields tangible improvements in regional resilience. We look forward to working closely with Director of Research Administration Beatrix Okonjo-Hale and the evaluation team from Fernlake Program Assessment to ensure rigorous monitoring of these collaborative outcomes.
