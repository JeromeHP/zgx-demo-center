## Data Management and Sharing Plan

The Calderwick Institute for Coastal Systems (CICS) is committed to ensuring that the observational and computational outputs generated by the *Compound Flood Forecasting for Under-Instrumented Estuaries* (CFFUE) project are managed with rigorous scientific integrity, accessibility, and long-term preservation. As a research institute within Alderbrook State University, CICS adheres to federal mandates and institutional policies regarding open science. This plan outlines the protocols for data handling, metadata documentation, repository deposition, and access restrictions throughout the three-year duration of the project, which commences on July 1, 2027.

### Data Types and Formats

The CFFUE project will generate two primary categories of data: high-resolution observational records from coastal monitoring stations and complex model output from sediment transport simulations. Observational data, including water level, salinity, and turbidity measurements, will be collected via instruments deployed in collaboration with the Rowan Tidal Observatory. These raw sensor readings will be processed into standardized CSV and NetCDF formats to ensure interoperability across different hydrodynamic modeling platforms.

Model output data, generated by Dr. Imogen Sarkissian’s team at CICS, will consist of time-series simulations and spatial grids depicting compound flooding scenarios. To facilitate reproducibility and future meta-analyses, model inputs, code scripts (Python/R), and resulting binary outputs will be version-controlled using Git. All final datasets will be archived in a format-independent structure, with detailed documentation provided for each variable, including units, sampling frequency, and processing algorithms. This approach ensures that the data remains usable beyond the immediate scope of the grant period.

### Metadata Standards

Consistent with NSF requirements and CICS internal guidelines, all datasets will be described using comprehensive metadata standards. Specifically, we will adopt the ISO 19115 standard for geographic information and adhere to the Dublin Core Metadata Initiative for general resource description. Metadata files will accompany each dataset deposit, detailing the provenance of the data, including instrument calibration logs, sensor placement coordinates, and model boundary conditions.

The metadata will be structured to include essential fields such as title, abstract, keywords, creator affiliations (including Dr. Imogen Sarkissian and Dr. Toma Weyland), and contact information for the corresponding author. By standardizing these descriptions, we enable efficient discovery and retrieval by other researchers in the ocean sciences community. Additionally, data dictionaries will be provided to explain any custom codes or flags used during data cleaning and quality control processes.

### Repository Deposit and Access

Upon completion of data processing, all datasets will be deposited in a trusted, domain-specific repository. We have selected the NSF-funded EarthCube data infrastructure for its robust support of geospatial and oceanographic data. The primary observational dataset will also be shared with the Quillon County Emergency Management agency, as indicated in our collaboration letter dated August 18, 2026, to support local resilience planning.

Access to the data will be managed through a tiered system. Primary datasets will be made publicly available upon publication of the associated peer-reviewed results, consistent with the outcomes of our prior award (CICS-FICT-OCE-2021), which successfully published three datasets under similar terms. Where sensitive information regarding critical infrastructure or private property boundaries is involved, data will be embargoed for a period of twelve months from the date of deposit to allow for analysis and protection of stakeholder interests. During the embargo period, access will be granted via secure request to verified academic and government partners.

### Long-Term Preservation

CICS ensures the long-term preservation of its research outputs through a formal partnership with the Alderbrook State University Library. Upon the conclusion of the project in 2030, all finalized datasets, metadata, and associated documentation will be transferred to the university’s institutional repository. This archive is committed to digital preservation standards, including regular backup routines and format migration strategies to prevent obsolescence.

The library’s data curation team will assign persistent identifiers (DOIs) to each dataset, ensuring they remain citable and traceable indefinitely. Furthermore, CICS will maintain a local copy of all raw and processed data for five years post-award, in accordance with NSF policy. This dual-layered preservation strategy—combining institutional repository support with specialized domain archives—guarantees that the scientific value of the CFFUE project endures, supporting future research in coastal hazard mitigation and estuarine dynamics.
