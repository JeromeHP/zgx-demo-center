## Project Overview and Administrative Context

This document serves as the Final Report for the prior award administered by the Calderwick Institute for Coastal Systems (CICS), under the leadership of Dr. Halvard Mensing, Director. The project, identified as CICS-FICT-OCE-2021 and titled "Sediment Transport Under Compound Flooding in Microtidal Estuaries," was funded by the NSF Division of Ocean Sciences with a total award amount of $842,600. The period of performance spanned from 2021-08-01 to 2025-07-31. This report details the scientific outcomes, personnel development, and operational challenges encountered during this five-year period, providing a transparent accounting of resources and results for the review panel.

The Principal Investigator, Dr. Imogen Sarkissian, Associate Director of Coastal Process Modelling at CICS, led the scientific effort in collaboration with Co-Principal Investigator Dr. Toma Weyland, Research Scientist in Sediment Dynamics. Administrative oversight was managed by Beatrix Okonjo-Hale, Director of Research Administration at CICS. The project operated within the context of CICS’s broader mission to study Mid-Atlantic estuarine and nearshore systems, leveraging the institute’s status as a research unit within Alderbrook State University.

## Scientific Outcomes and Dissemination

The project successfully met its core scientific objectives regarding the characterization of sediment dynamics during compound flooding events. Despite operational hurdles in field data collection, the team achieved significant outputs in peer-reviewed literature and open science resources. The final tally of scholarly output includes 9 peer-reviewed papers published in high-impact journals focusing on hydrodynamic modeling, sediment flux analysis, and coastal resilience. These publications have contributed substantially to the understanding of microtidal estuarine behavior under complex forcing conditions.

In alignment with NSF data management policies, the team made 3 published datasets publicly available through institutional repositories. These datasets include time-series measurements from instrumented sites and model validation grids, ensuring reproducibility for the broader scientific community. The digital preservation of these records supports future meta-analyses and enhances the utility of the investment in this research domain.

## Personnel Development and Training

A key metric of success for this award was the training of early-career scientists. The project directly supported 4 graduate students, providing them with hands-on experience in field instrumentation, data processing, and scientific writing. These trainees gained critical skills in coastal process modeling and sediment dynamics, positioning them for successful careers in academia and government agencies. The mentorship provided by Dr. Sarkissian and Dr. Weyland ensured that the educational component of the grant was robust, with all supported students completing their degrees or advancing to postdoctoral positions within CICS or partner institutions.

## Operational Challenges and Field Campaigns

While the theoretical and analytical components of the project proceeded as planned, field operations faced significant disruptions. The initial proposal outlined 8 planned field campaigns designed to capture a comprehensive range of hydrodynamic conditions. However, only 5 of 8 planned field campaigns were successfully completed by the end date of 2025-07-31. This shortfall represents a critical gap in the temporal coverage of the dataset, particularly regarding extreme event characterization.

The reduction in field campaigns was driven by three distinct and compounding factors:
1. Two storm seasons were lost entirely due to vessel availability issues. Unforeseen logistical constraints and charter cancellations prevented the deployment of research vessels during two critical high-energy periods, resulting in missed opportunities for data collection during peak compound flooding events.
2. One field campaign was canceled due to equipment loss. A major sensor array was lost overboard during a preliminary deployment, requiring extensive procurement and recalibration time that delayed the subsequent scheduled campaign beyond the viable weather window.

Despite these setbacks, the team maximized the utility of the available data. The 5 completed campaigns yielded 22 instrument deployments across key estuarine nodes. These deployments provided high-resolution data on bedload transport and suspended sediment concentration during moderate to high-flow conditions. While the absence of two extreme-event datasets limits the generalizability of the findings regarding peak sediment flux, the remaining data remains scientifically valuable for calibrating numerical models under non-extreme compound flooding scenarios.

## Transition to Future Work

The insights gained from CICS-FICT-OCE-2021 have directly informed the development of a new proposal, "Compound Flood Forecasting for Under-Instrumented Estuaries" (CFFUE). This new project, targeting NSF solicitation 26-516 under the Geosciences Core Research program, seeks to address the data gaps identified in this final report. Specifically, CFFUE aims to develop predictive models that can compensate for sparse instrumentation, reducing reliance on continuous field campaigns.

The CFFUE proposal, led by Dr. Sarkissian and Dr. Weyland, requests $1,029,198 over three years (starting 2027-07-01). It builds upon the methodological foundations established in this prior award while incorporating lessons learned from its operational challenges. The new project has secured letters of support from key collaborators, including Rowan Tidal Observatory and Quillon County Emergency Management, demonstrating strong community and institutional engagement.

## Conclusion

The "Sediment Transport Under Compound Flooding in Microtidal Estuaries" project delivered substantial scientific value through 9 peer-reviewed papers, 3 published datasets, and the training of 4 graduate students. While the completion of only 5 of 8 planned field campaigns represents a shortfall due to vessel availability and equipment loss, the resulting 22 instrument deployments provided critical insights into estuarine sediment dynamics. The Calderwick Institute for Coastal Systems remains committed to advancing coastal science through rigorous research and responsible stewardship of grant resources. The outcomes of this award lay the groundwork for more resilient and efficient future investigations into compound flooding impacts on coastal systems.
