## Project Summary and Institutional Context

This Final Progress Report summarizes the outcomes of NIH R01 award VSU-FICT-R01-2021, titled *Mitochondrial Dynamics in Skeletal Muscle Insulin Resistance*. The project was conducted at Vantcross State University (Vantcross), a public R1 doctoral university with very high research activity located in the Upper Midwest. Vantcross operates eleven colleges and maintains an annual research expenditure of $412,000,000. The Office of Sponsored Programs, led by Executive Director Corwin Blathwaite, oversees the administration of this award under the direction of PI Dr. Rurik Anselmi and Co-PI Dr. Pernille Adeyemo-Strand.

The primary objective of this five-year award (April 1, 2021 – March 31, 2026) was to elucidate the mechanistic links between mitochondrial fission/fusion dynamics and insulin signaling pathways in skeletal muscle tissue. This report details the scientific outcomes, personnel training, and resource utilization achieved during the final period of performance.

## Scientific Outcomes and Specific Aims Status

The project addressed three specific aims. We successfully completed two of the three proposed aims. The third aim was not completed due to unforeseen technical challenges that extended beyond our control.

**Aim 1: Characterization of Mitochondrial Morphology in Insulin-Resistant Models.**
This aim was fully achieved. We established a robust protocol for high-resolution imaging of mitochondrial networks in mouse skeletal muscle. Data generated from this aim have been integrated into three peer-reviewed publications and two deposited datasets.

**Aim 2: Quantification of Fission/Fusion Protein Expression.**
This aim was also completed. We identified significant dysregulation in Drp1 and Mfn2 expression levels in high-fat-diet-induced insulin resistance models. These findings were disseminated through four peer-reviewed papers and presented at two national conferences.

**Aim 3: Functional Validation via Tissue-Specific Knockout.**
This aim remains incomplete. The objective was to validate causal relationships using a muscle-specific knockout line for the fission protein Drp1. However, the generation of this knockout model encountered critical failures. The primary knockout line failed to breed successfully due to embryonic lethality associated with the genetic construct, necessitating the development of a secondary line. Concurrently, we experienced an eighteen-month delay in the availability of core instrument capacity at the Pellworth Metabolic Core, which was required for the metabolic phenotyping of these animals. This dual bottleneck prevented the completion of functional validation within the award period. We acknowledge this shortfall and have incorporated lessons learned into our current proposal for *Mitochondrial Quality Control as a Determinant of Muscle Insulin Sensitivity* (MQC), where we have secured guaranteed access to alternative instrumentation.

## Dissemination and Training Outcomes

Despite the shortfall in Aim 3, the project yielded significant scholarly output and trained the next generation of scientists in metabolic physiology.

**Publications:**
The project resulted in **11 peer-reviewed papers**. These publications appear in high-impact journals including *Cell Metabolism*, *Journal of Biological Chemistry*, and *Diabetes*. Additionally, we have 19 papers currently in draft or preparation stages that build directly upon this data. The dissemination of these findings was further amplified through **7 invited talks** delivered by the PI and Co-PI at national symposia and international conferences.

**Trainee Development:**
We supported **5 trainees** during the life of this award, including two doctoral candidates who successfully defended their dissertations based on this work, and three postdoctoral fellows who have since secured independent positions. While our internal tracking indicates that 9 trainees were nominally associated with the grant, five received direct support through salary or stipend funds administered by Vantcross Sponsored Programs.

**Data Management:**
Two major datasets generated from Aim 1 and Aim 2 have been deposited in public repositories. These datasets include raw imaging data and proteomic profiles, ensuring reproducibility and facilitating future meta-analyses.

## Conclusion and Future Directions

While we did not complete all specific aims due to technical breeding failures and core facility delays, the data generated from Aims 1 and 2 provide a strong foundation for our next phase of inquiry. The incomplete Aim 3 highlights the necessity of robust contingency planning in genetic studies, a lesson directly applied to our upcoming resubmission, MQC.

We are grateful for the support provided by the NIH and the collaborative efforts of our internal teams at Vantcross State University. The outcomes reported herein reflect a rigorous scientific effort that has advanced our understanding of mitochondrial dynamics in insulin resistance, despite the operational challenges encountered in the final year of the award.
