## Core Facilities and Shared Instrumentation

Vantcross State University (Vantcross) operates a comprehensive suite of shared research infrastructure designed to support the high-impact, multidisciplinary investigations required at a public R1 doctoral university with very high research activity. With an annual research expenditure of $412 million and a research staff of 1,840 individuals, the institution maintains five primary core facilities that serve both internal investigators across its eleven colleges and eligible external collaborators. These facilities are managed under strict recharge-rate models to ensure sustainability, instrument maintenance, and technical expertise availability. The following sections detail the capabilities, operational arrangements, and access policies for each core service relevant to metabolic phenotyping, genomics, imaging, biostatistics, and computational resources.

### Metabolic Phenotyping Core

The Vantcross Metabolic Phenotyping Core provides advanced physiological assessment services essential for studies involving energy metabolism, glucose homeostasis, and lipid profiles. The facility is equipped with indirect calorimetry systems, hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp setups, and stable isotope tracer analysis capabilities. Access to the core is governed by a recharge-rate agreement that covers instrument time, reagent costs, and technical support. Internal users from Vantcross colleges are billed at a subsidized rate reflecting 70% of the full operating cost, while external users pay the full commercial recharge rate. The core maintains a staff of three full-time metabolic physiologists and two research technicians who provide protocol design consultation and data interpretation assistance. Letters of collaboration confirm guaranteed priority access for ongoing NIH-funded projects involving mitochondrial dynamics and insulin resistance mechanisms.

### Genomics and Sequencing Core

The Genomics and Sequencing Core supports high-throughput molecular analysis through next-generation sequencing (NGS) platforms, including whole-genome, exome, and RNA-seq workflows. The facility houses Illumina NovaSeq and MiSeq instruments, alongside automated library preparation stations. Recharge rates are structured to cover consumables, sequencing reagents, and bioinformatics pipeline maintenance. Internal users receive a 15% discount on per-sample fees, whereas external academic institutions pay the standard rate. The core collaborates closely with the Biostatistics Consulting Service to ensure data quality control prior to downstream analysis. For projects requiring multi-omics integration, the core offers bundled service packages that include sample preparation, sequencing, and initial raw data processing.

### Cryo-Electron Microscopy Facility

The Cryo-EM facility provides high-resolution structural biology services using two Titan Krios microscopes operating at 300 kV. The facility is equipped with automated data collection systems and direct electron detectors, enabling single-particle analysis and cryo-electron tomography. Due to the specialized nature of the instrumentation, access is limited by appointment and subject to a significant recharge fee that accounts for high electricity consumption, liquid nitrogen supply, and rigorous maintenance schedules. Internal users are charged at a reduced rate that covers only direct operating costs, while external users pay the full commercial rate. The facility’s technical staff includes two senior microscopists who provide training on sample vitrification and grid preparation. Collaboration letters indicate dedicated instrument time for projects requiring structural validation of protein complexes involved in metabolic regulation.

### Biostatistics Consulting Service

The Biostatistics Consulting Service offers expert guidance on study design, power analysis, and statistical modeling for complex research datasets. Unlike the instrumental cores, this service operates on a fee-for-consultation model rather than a strict recharge rate tied to physical assets. Consultants are available to assist with experimental design, randomization strategies, and advanced analytical methods such as mixed-effects models and survival analysis. Internal faculty members receive hourly billing at a nominal institutional rate, while external entities are billed at professional consulting rates. The service plays a critical role in ensuring rigorous data interpretation for genomics and metabolic studies, particularly in the context of multi-center collaborations.

### High-Performance Computing (HPC) Cluster

Vantcross’s HPC cluster provides substantial computational resources for data-intensive research, including genomic alignment, molecular dynamics simulations, and large-scale statistical modeling. The system comprises over 10,000 CPU cores and dedicated GPU nodes for machine learning applications. Access is managed through a proposal-based allocation system. Internal users submit resource requests via the university’s research computing portal, with costs offset by institutional subsidies. External users may access the cluster through collaborative agreements, paying a per-core-hour rate that reflects the full cost of hardware depreciation, power, and cooling. The HPC team provides technical support for software installation and job optimization, ensuring efficient utilization of computational assets.

### External Access Policy

While Vantcross prioritizes service to its internal research community, several facilities are open to external users under specific conditions. The Metabolic Phenotyping Core, Genomics Core, and Cryo-EM facility accept applications from non-affiliated researchers who demonstrate scientific merit and have secured funding for the associated recharge fees. External access is subject to instrument availability and requires a signed service agreement outlining liability, data ownership, and confidentiality terms. The Biostatistics Consulting Service and HPC cluster offer limited external support through formal collaboration agreements, ensuring that external partnerships contribute fairly to operational costs. All facilities adhere to strict safety and compliance protocols, with regular audits conducted by the Office of Sponsored Programs to ensure alignment with institutional policies and federal regulations.
