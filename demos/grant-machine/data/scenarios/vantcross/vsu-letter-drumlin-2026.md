## Collaboration Scope and Analytical Framework

The Drumlin Analytical Institute confirms its commitment to serving as the primary computational partner for the proposed project, *Mitochondrial Quality Control as a Determinant of Muscle Insulin Sensitivity* (MQC). Our collaboration focuses on the integration, normalization, and statistical modeling of multi-omics datasets generated across the study’s three specific aims. Specifically, Drumlin will manage the bioinformatic pipeline for single-nucleus RNA sequencing data and perform longitudinal mixed-effects modeling to assess mitochondrial dynamics in skeletal muscle tissue.

## Personnel Commitment

To ensure rigorous analytical oversight, we have assigned a dedicated team of senior researchers with expertise in metabolic genomics and statistical biology. Dr. Elena Voss, Lead Computational Biologist, will supervise the entire data integration workflow, dedicating 4.5 months of effort annually to this project. She will be supported by Mr. Julian Kael, Senior Data Engineer, who will maintain the high-performance computing infrastructure required for large-scale transcriptomic analysis (3.0 months/year). Additionally, Dr. Aris Thorne, Statistician, will design the power calculations and execute the primary statistical analyses for Aim 2 and Aim 3, committing 2.5 months of effort per year.

This team structure ensures that all analytical components are handled by personnel with established track records in mitochondrial physiology data analysis. The collective expertise of the Drumlin team addresses the previous critique regarding underspecified statistical plans by providing a robust, pre-validated analytical framework tailored to the MQC study design.

## Data Management and Integration

Drumlin will receive raw data files from the Pellworth Metabolic Core and Vantcross State University laboratories on a monthly basis during the active phase of the grant (July 2027 through June 2030). All data will be processed according to NIH Data Management and Sharing Plan standards. We will utilize secure, encrypted servers hosted at the Drumlin Institute for intermediate storage, with final datasets deposited in the designated public repositories upon publication or at the conclusion of the grant period, whichever comes first.

Access controls will be strictly managed to protect any human-derived data in compliance with IRB protocols. The Drumlin team will provide quarterly progress reports on analytical milestones to the Principal Investigator, Dr. Rurik Anselmi, and Co-Principal Investigator, Dr. Pernille Adeyemo-Strand. These reports will detail data quality metrics, pipeline validation status, and preliminary statistical findings to inform iterative experimental design adjustments.

## Resource Availability

The Drumlin Analytical Institute has sufficient computational capacity and personnel bandwidth to support the MQC project without conflict with other ongoing commitments. Our high-performance computing cluster is currently underutilized relative to projected MQC workloads, ensuring that data processing will not experience delays due to infrastructure bottlenecks. We are prepared to begin analytical workflows immediately upon receipt of initial datasets in late 2027.

We look forward to a productive collaboration that advances our understanding of mitochondrial quality control mechanisms in metabolic disease.
