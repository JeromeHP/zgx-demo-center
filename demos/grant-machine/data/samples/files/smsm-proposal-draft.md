# Stepped Mobilization and Sedation Minimization in Critical Illness (SMSM)

A multi-site randomized clinical trial submitted to the National Institutes of Health under
PA-25-305, NIH Research Project Grant (Parent R01 Clinical Trial Required).

## Specific Aims

Prolonged sedation and immobility in mechanically ventilated patients contribute to
long-term physical and cognitive impairment. This trial tests whether a stepped
protocol combining early mobilization with structured sedation minimization reduces
ventilator-free days compared with usual care.

**Primary aim.** Compare ventilator-free days at day 28 between arms.

**Secondary aims.** Compare ICU delirium incidence, six-month physical function, and
hospital length of stay.

## Results from Prior Support

Under NIH award AUMC-FICT-R01-2021, "Early Mobilization Protocols in Mechanically
Ventilated Patients" ($2,640,000, 2021–2026), the team enrolled 288 participants at 4
sites and published 6 peer-reviewed papers. The trial is registered as NCT-FICT-2021-014
and reached primary completion on 30 June 2025.

## Study Design

A pragmatic, multi-site, parallel-group randomized trial. Participants will be
randomized 1:1 to the stepped protocol or usual care. Target enrollment is 600
participants across three sites (Ashgrove, Wrenfield Regional Hospital, Calloway Valley
Health) over five years.

## Enrollment Feasibility

Electronic health record screening supports enrollment of 20 participants per site per
month, which comfortably supports the target within the proposed period.

## Human Subjects Protection

The study will be conducted under the institution's IRB, which has approved the parent
protocol. Approval is current and in good standing. Informed consent will be obtained
from patients or their legally authorized representatives.

## Data and Safety Monitoring

An independent data safety monitoring board is in place and will review accumulating
safety data at six-month intervals. Stopping rules for efficacy and futility are
prespecified.

## Statistical Analysis

The primary analysis will compare ventilator-free days between arms using a Wilcoxon
rank-sum test, with a sample size providing 80% power to detect a 1.5-day difference.

## Data Sharing

De-identified participant-level data will be deposited in a controlled-access
repository following publication of the primary results.
