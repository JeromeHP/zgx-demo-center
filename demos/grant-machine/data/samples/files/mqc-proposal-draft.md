# Mitochondrial Quality Control as a Determinant of Muscle Insulin Sensitivity

Resubmission (A1) to the National Institutes of Health under PA-25-301, NIH Research
Project Grant (Parent R01 Clinical Trial Not Allowed).

## Specific Aims

Skeletal muscle insulin resistance is a central lesion in type 2 diabetes, and
mitochondrial dysfunction is strongly implicated. Our prior work established that
mitochondrial fission dynamics track insulin sensitivity across a human cohort. This
proposal tests whether mitochondrial quality control — specifically mitophagic flux —
is a causal determinant rather than a correlate.

**Aim 1.** Quantify mitophagic flux in muscle biopsies across a metabolically
characterized human cohort.

**Aim 2.** Determine whether genetic manipulation of mitophagy in murine muscle alters
insulin sensitivity.

**Aim 3.** Test whether a pharmacological mitophagy inducer restores insulin signalling
in a diet-induced obesity model.

## Results from Prior Support

Under NIH award VSU-FICT-R01-2021, "Mitochondrial Dynamics in Skeletal Muscle Insulin
Resistance" ($1,980,000, 2021–2026), the laboratory published 19 peer-reviewed papers
and supported 5 trainees. Two of the three specific aims were completed; the third was
delayed by a knockout line that failed to breed. The findings have been disseminated
through 7 invited talks.

## Approach

### Aim 1 approach

Biopsies will be obtained from 120 participants stratified by insulin sensitivity.
Mitophagic flux will be assessed by immunoblot and confocal imaging.

### Aim 2 approach

Muscle-specific knockout and overexpression lines will be generated and phenotyped by
hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp.

### Aim 3 approach

Diet-induced obese mice will receive the compound or vehicle, followed by metabolic
phenotyping.

Statistical analysis will be performed by the co-investigator.
