## Senior Personnel Justification

The success of the Smart Automation Technician Pathway (SATP) relies on a dedicated leadership team with proven expertise in workforce development, curriculum design, and industry engagement. The project is led by Dr. Renee Okafor, Dean of Workforce and Technical Programs at Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC). As Principal Investigator, Dr. Okafor provides strategic oversight for the integration of automation technologies into our credit and non-credit programs. With a background in adult learning and technical education, she ensures that SATP aligns with HCCC’s mission to serve our four rural counties in the Mid-South. Her role includes managing the overall budget, ensuring compliance with NSF ATE guidelines, and facilitating partnerships with local industry stakeholders.

Co-Principal Investigator Marcus Lindqvist, Chair of the Mechatronics and Industrial Automation department, brings deep technical expertise to the project. Mr. Lindqvist has been instrumental in designing the hands-on laboratory components of our curriculum and has established strong ties with regional manufacturers. His primary responsibilities include supervising the development of new instructional modules, overseeing student lab safety protocols, and coordinating with industry partners to ensure that equipment training meets current manufacturing standards.

Supporting this leadership team is Dana Whitfield, Director of Grants and Sponsored Programs. Ms. Whitfield serves as the administrative manager for SATP, handling all financial reporting, procurement processes, and compliance documentation. Her experience in managing federal grants ensures that HCCC maintains rigorous fiscal accountability throughout the three-year project lifecycle (July 1, 2027 – June 30, 2030).

## Other Personnel Justification

The SATP budget allocates resources for part-time instructional staff and student assistants essential to delivering high-quality technical training. Two adjunct faculty members will be hired to teach specialized modules in robotic programming and PLC (Programmable Logic Controller) integration. These instructors are selected based on their current industry certifications and experience, ensuring that students receive instruction that reflects real-world manufacturing environments.

Additionally, three undergraduate student assistants from HCCC’s Engineering Technology program will be employed during the academic semesters. These students will support lab setup, assist with peer tutoring sessions, and help maintain inventory for training materials. This employment opportunity not only reduces operational costs but also provides our own students with early exposure to professional technical workspaces.

## Fringe Benefits Justification

Fringe benefits are calculated at a flat rate of 28.0% of total personnel costs (senior and other personnel). This rate reflects the college’s current internal budgeting assumptions for the project period. While HCCC maintains a negotiated indirect cost agreement with the federal government that includes a fringe benefit component, this specific grant budget utilizes the simplified 28.0% calculation to streamline financial administration. The benefits covered under this rate include health insurance contributions, retirement plan matching, and payroll taxes as applicable to part-time and adjunct staff.

## Equipment Justification

The SATP project requires significant investment in modern automation hardware to replace outdated instructional tools. Budgeted equipment includes three collaborative robot arms (cobots), two programmable logic controller training kits, and one industrial vision system simulator. These items are critical for providing students with hands-on experience using technology currently deployed by our industry partners. The equipment will be housed in the newly renovated Mechatronics Lab at HCCC’s main campus. As per NSF guidelines, these items are capitalized as project assets and will remain under HCCC’s stewardship post-award.

## Travel Justification

Travel funds are allocated to support two primary activities: faculty development and industry partnership meetings. Two round-trip flights per year for Dr. Okafor and Mr. Lindqvist are budgeted to attend the NSF ATE National Conference and the Advanced Manufacturing Association Annual Summit. These conferences provide essential professional development opportunities, allowing our team to stay current with pedagogical best practices and emerging automation trends.

Additionally, local travel is budgeted for quarterly meetings with our advisory board members and industry partners. Given HCCC’s service area of four rural counties, travel distances can be significant. The budget accounts for mileage reimbursement for site visits to partner facilities, including Kessler Fluid Systems and Tri-County Packaging Cooperative, to assess equipment needs and review student placement outcomes.

## Participant Support Justification

Participant support costs are designed to remove financial barriers for non-traditional students enrolling in the SATP pathway. Funds will be used to provide stipends to up to 15 students per year who demonstrate financial need but do not qualify for full Pell Grant coverage. These stipends cover textbook costs, certification exam fees (such as Siemens or Rockwell Automation credentials), and transportation assistance. By directly supporting student expenses, we aim to increase completion rates beyond the target of 150 completers over three years. Note that participant support is excluded from the Modified Total Direct Cost (MTDC) base for indirect cost calculations.

## Materials and Supplies Justification

The materials and supplies category covers consumable items required for daily laboratory operations. This includes raw materials for prototyping (such as aluminum stock, wiring harnesses, and pneumatic components), safety gear (gloves, goggles, ear protection), and software licenses for simulation platforms. These supplies are essential for maintaining the integrity of hands-on training exercises. The budget assumes a steady consumption rate aligned with the enrollment targets for each academic year.

## Evaluation Contract Justification

HCCC has contracted with Bluestem Evaluation Group to provide independent, external evaluation of the SATP project. Dr. Priya Raman, Lead Evaluator at Bluestem, brings extensive experience in assessing STEM workforce development initiatives. The evaluation contract covers formative assessments during the first two years and a summative evaluation in the final year. Services include data collection instrument design, annual progress reporting, and a final impact analysis measuring student placement rates and employer satisfaction. This independent evaluation ensures objectivity and provides credible evidence of project effectiveness for future funding cycles.

## Other Direct Costs Justification

Other direct costs include web hosting services for the project’s dissemination portal, copyright fees for adapted curriculum materials, and conference registration fees for presenting project outcomes at regional education forums. These costs are necessary to ensure that SATP’s resources are widely disseminated to other community colleges and workforce development agencies across the Mid-South.

## Indirect Costs Justification

Indirect costs are calculated at a rate of 36.0% of the Modified Total Direct Cost (MTDC) base. The MTDC base includes all direct salaries, fringe benefits, materials, supplies, travel, equipment, and other direct costs, with the exception of participant support. This calculation aligns with HCCC’s current indirect cost agreement with the federal government (document: rate-agreement-fy2026). These funds support institutional infrastructure, including library services, facilities maintenance, and administrative oversight, which are essential for the successful execution of the project.

## Institutional Context and Sustainability

Harrow Creek Community College is a public two-year institution serving four rural counties in the Mid-South. With a fall 2025 credit headcount of 6,840, HCCC serves a diverse student population, including 52% Pell Grant recipients and 41% first-generation college students. The SATP project addresses critical workforce gaps in these communities by preparing students for high-demand careers in automation and mechatronics.

The project builds on the foundation of our prior NSF ATE award, HCCC-FICT-ATE-2021: "Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers" (2021–2025). That project successfully trained 84 completers against a target of 120, achieving a 78% placement rate among respondents. It also established relationships with 11 industry partners and developed three new courses. SATP leverages these existing partnerships, including letters of support from Kessler Fluid Systems (dated August 12, 2026) and Tri-County Packaging Cooperative (dated August 20, 2026), to ensure that our curriculum remains responsive to industry needs.

By combining rigorous technical training with robust student support services, SATP aims to create a sustainable pathway for rural students to enter the advanced manufacturing sector. The projected total budget of $1,038,450 over three years represents a strategic investment in both human capital and institutional infrastructure, positioning HCCC as a leader in workforce development for the Mid-South region.
