## Executive Summary

This document serves as the Final Outcomes Report for the National Science Foundation Advanced Technological Education (NSF ATE) award HCCC-FICT-ATE-2021, titled *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers*. The project period spanned from September 1, 2021, to August 31, 2025. Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC), a public two-year institution serving four rural counties in the Mid-South, executed this initiative under the leadership of Principal Investigator Dr. Renee Okafor, Dean of Workforce and Technical Programs, with Co-Principal Investigator Marcus Lindqvist, Chair of Mechatronics and Industrial Automation.

The primary objective of the award was to develop and implement a sustainable pathway for students entering the mechatronics workforce, specifically targeting employers in rural manufacturing sectors. While the project successfully established foundational curricula, engaged significant industry partners, and achieved notable completion numbers, it did not meet its initial target for student completers. This report provides an honest accounting of these outcomes, detailing the quantitative results, contextual factors influencing enrollment, and the structural assets created during the five-year period.

## Project Goals and Institutional Context

HCCC operates with a mission deeply rooted in serving its regional community, where 52% of the student body receives Pell Grants and 41% are first-generation college students. As of Fall 2025, the institution maintains a credit headcount of 6,840. The *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* project was designed to address the specific skills gap in local manufacturing by aligning technical education with the needs of rural employers.

The project budget totaled $598,112, funded entirely through the NSF ATE program. Oversight of the financial and administrative aspects was managed by Dana Whitfield, Director of Grants and Sponsored Programs, ensuring compliance with federal regulations and institutional policies. The evaluation of the project’s impact was conducted by Dr. Priya Raman of Bluestem Evaluation Group, providing an independent assessment of the program’s efficacy and reach.

## Quantitative Outcomes: Completions and Placement

The most critical metric for this award was the number of students completing the mechatronics certificate or degree pathway. The original proposal set a target of 120 completers over the five-year period. The final data indicates that 84 students successfully completed the program. This represents 70% of the targeted goal.

While the shortfall is notable, it is essential to contextualize this figure within the broader landscape of workforce development and the specific challenges faced during the project timeline. Of the 84 completers, employment outcomes were tracked via survey. A placement rate of 78% was recorded among the 61 respondents who completed the post-completion survey. This indicates that a significant majority of those who finished the program secured employment in fields related to their training, validating the quality and relevance of the curriculum despite the lower volume of graduates.

## Analysis of Shortfall: Enrollment and Operational Challenges

An honest assessment of the project’s performance requires an examination of the factors that contributed to the gap between the 120-completer target and the actual 84 completers. The evaluation conducted by Dr. Priya Raman identified two primary drivers for this variance: macro-environmental enrollment declines and specific operational disruptions within the college.

### COVID-Era Enrollment Declines

The first two years of the project (2021-2023) coincided with the tail end of the global pandemic and its immediate aftermath. During this period, HCCC experienced a broader institutional trend of enrollment declines across technical and workforce programs. Potential students faced economic instability, health concerns, and shifting priorities that reduced their willingness or ability to enroll in rigorous technical training. This external factor significantly dampened the applicant pool for the mechatronics pathway during its critical launch phase. The decline was not unique to HCCC but reflected a regional pattern in the Mid-South, where rural communities faced compounded economic pressures.

### Instructor Vacancy and Course Delays

A more specific operational challenge arose within the Department of Mechatronics and Industrial Automation. An unexpected instructor vacancy occurred during the third year of the project (2023-2024). This position was critical for delivering core sequence courses required for program completion. The delay in filling this vacancy resulted in a pause in course offerings for approximately one academic year.

For students already enrolled, this created bottlenecks in their progression through the curriculum. Many were forced to defer their enrollment or reduce their course loads, extending the time to completion. For new applicants, the lack of available sections discouraged enrollment, as the program’s continuity appeared uncertain. This operational hiccup effectively compressed the window for student accumulation, contributing directly to the shortfall in final completers.

## Programmatic Assets and Industry Engagement

Despite the missed target on completions, the project succeeded in building substantial infrastructure and partnerships that will support future workforce development efforts. The following assets were established during the 2021-2025 period:

### Curriculum Development

The project team developed three new courses specifically designed to address gaps in rural manufacturing skill sets. These courses integrated theoretical knowledge with hands-on application, focusing on automation systems, predictive maintenance, and digital fabrication. These courses are now embedded in HCCC’s permanent curriculum, ensuring that future cohorts of students will have access to high-quality, relevant instruction. The creation of these courses was led by Marcus Lindqvist and his team, incorporating feedback from industry partners to ensure alignment with employer needs.

### Industry Partnerships

A key goal of the NSF ATE award was to strengthen ties between the college and local manufacturers. Over the five-year period, HCCC engaged 11 industry partners. These partnerships ranged from advisory board participation to direct input on curriculum design. The engagement fostered a collaborative environment where employers could voice specific skill requirements, allowing the college to tailor its training more effectively.

Notable among these partners was Northgate Precision Castings, which provided early support and validation for the program’s direction. Their involvement, documented in a letter of support dated March 15, 2021, helped legitimize the initiative within the regional manufacturing community. Other partners contributed to internships, equipment donations, and guest lectures, enriching the student experience.

### Dual Credit Agreements

To broaden access to the program and introduce high school students to mechatronics concepts earlier, HCCC signed two dual credit agreements during the project period. These agreements allowed eligible high school students to earn college credits while still in secondary education, effectively lowering the barrier to entry for the pathway. This strategy helped build a pipeline of interested students, although the impact was partially mitigated by the broader enrollment declines mentioned earlier.

## Sustainability and Future Directions

The *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* project has laid a strong foundation for continued growth in technical education at HCCC. The three new courses, 11 industry partnerships, and dual credit agreements are permanent assets that will continue to benefit students long after the grant period ended.

Building on this momentum, HCCC is preparing to launch a new initiative titled *Smart Automation Technician Pathway* (SATP). This project, which begins in July 2027, aims to expand upon the successes of the previous award while addressing its limitations. With a target of 150 completers and a budget of $1,038,450, the SATP initiative seeks to capitalize on the established partnerships and curriculum improvements.

Letters of support from key employers such as Kessler Fluid Systems (dated August 12, 2026) and Tri-County Packaging Cooperative (dated August 20, 2026) indicate strong continued interest in HCCC’s technical training programs. These letters specifically reference the SATP project, signaling that industry partners are eager to engage with the next phase of workforce development.

## Conclusion

The *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* award achieved significant milestones in curriculum development, industry engagement, and student placement. While the completion rate fell short of the 120-target due to unforeseen external and internal challenges, the program demonstrated resilience and adaptability. The 78% placement rate among respondents underscores the value of the training provided.

The lessons learned from this project—particularly regarding enrollment volatility and the critical importance of stable staffing—will inform the design and implementation of future initiatives. HCCC remains committed to serving its rural community and supporting the manufacturing sector through high-quality, accessible technical education. The assets created during this period ensure that the college is well-positioned to meet the evolving needs of its students and employers in the years to come.
