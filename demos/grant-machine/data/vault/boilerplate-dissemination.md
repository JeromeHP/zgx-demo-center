## Dissemination Strategy Overview

Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC) is committed to maximizing the impact of National Science Foundation Advanced Technological Education (ATE) funded projects through a robust, multi-tiered dissemination plan. As a public two-year institution serving four rural counties in the Mid-South, HCCC recognizes that effective knowledge transfer is critical for scaling workforce development innovations. Our strategy leverages existing institutional infrastructure, established industry partnerships, and proven digital platforms to ensure that project outcomes reach educators, industry stakeholders, policymakers, and the broader educational community. This approach builds upon the successful dissemination framework utilized in our prior award, *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* (HCCC-FICT-ATE-2021), which successfully engaged 11 industry partners and resulted in the adoption of three new courses across regional institutions.

## Digital Platforms and Open Educational Resources

A cornerstone of HCCC’s dissemination strategy is the strategic use of digital channels to ensure broad, equitable access to project materials. The college’s ATE program website serves as a central hub for hosting open-licensed curriculum, instructional videos, assessment tools, and implementation guides. By adhering to open licensing standards, we remove barriers to entry for other institutions seeking to adapt our models for their local contexts. This digital repository will be continuously updated throughout the three-year duration of the *Smart Automation Technician Pathway* (SATP) project, with major milestones triggering specific content releases.

Furthermore, HCCC utilizes its institutional learning management system and professional development portals to distribute resources directly to faculty members within our service area. With a student body comprising 52% Pell Grant recipients and 41% first-generation students, we prioritize accessible, user-friendly digital formats that accommodate diverse learning needs and technical proficiencies. The dissemination team, overseen by Grants Director Dana Whitfield, will ensure that all uploaded materials are metadata-tagged for discoverability across national ATE project centers and regional educational networks.

## Conference Presentations and Professional Networks

HCCC actively participates in national and regional professional conferences to share findings with the broader STEM education community. Project leads Dr. Renee Okafor, Dean of Workforce and Technical Programs, and Marcus Lindqvist, Chair of Mechatronics and Industrial Automation, are committed to presenting project outcomes at key venues such as the National ATE Conference, the American Society for Engineering Education (ASEE) Annual Conference, and relevant regional technical education summits. These presentations will highlight both quantitative metrics, such as our target of 150 completers, and qualitative insights gained from engaging with employers like Kessler Fluid Systems and Tri-County Packaging Cooperative.

In addition to formal conference presentations, HCCC faculty will contribute articles to peer-reviewed journals and industry trade publications. These written contributions will focus on best practices for integrating smart automation curricula into community college pathways, offering actionable strategies for other institutions facing similar workforce challenges in rural and semi-rural settings.

## Regional Stakeholder Engagement and Policy Briefings

Beyond the academic community, HCCC prioritizes direct engagement with regional workforce boards, economic development agencies, and local policymakers. We will conduct quarterly briefings for the Four Rural Counties Workforce Development Board, presenting data on student placement rates, industry demand signals, and curriculum efficacy. These briefings are designed to inform policy decisions and secure sustained local support for technical education initiatives.

Our prior experience demonstrates the value of this approach; the *Mechatronics Pathways* project resulted in two dual-credit agreements and a 78% placement rate among respondents, insights that were shared extensively with local employers including Northgate Precision Castings. For the SATP project, we will continue this tradition by producing concise policy briefs and one-page fact sheets tailored for non-academic audiences. These materials will emphasize the economic return on investment for community technical training and the alignment of HCCC’s programs with regional manufacturing needs. By maintaining transparent communication with these stakeholders, HCCC ensures that our dissemination efforts are not only broad in reach but also deeply embedded in the local economic ecosystem.
