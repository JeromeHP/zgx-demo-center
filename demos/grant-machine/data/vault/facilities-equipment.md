## Existing Facilities and Equipment Overview

Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC), a public two-year institution serving four rural counties in the Mid-South, maintains a robust infrastructure dedicated to workforce development and technical education. With a fall 2025 credit headcount of 6,840 students—of whom 52% are Pell Grant recipients and 41% are first-generation college attendees—HCCC recognizes that high-quality, hands-on training is essential for student success. The proposed Smart Automation Technician Pathway (SATP) will leverage the college’s existing Mechatronics and Industrial Automation facilities, which have been rigorously upgraded through prior NSF ATE funding. These resources provide a stable foundation for delivering industry-aligned curriculum and preparing students for careers in rural manufacturing and automation sectors.

## Prior Award Legacy and Infrastructure Development

The core of HCCC’s automation training capabilities stems from the successful completion of the prior award, *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* (HCCC-FICT-ATE-2021), funded by NSF ATE from September 1, 2021, to August 31, 2025. This three-year initiative, led by Dr. Renee Okafor, Dean of Workforce and Technical Programs, resulted in the acquisition of state-of-the-art equipment and the development of three new courses tailored to regional employer needs. The prior project engaged 11 industry partners and established dual-credit agreements with two local high schools, significantly expanding the pipeline of interested students. Although the project achieved a completion rate of 84 out of 120 targeted completers and a 78% placement rate among respondents, the physical infrastructure remains fully operational and is central to the SATP’s strategy for scaling impact.

## The Mechatronics Automation Lab

HCCC’s primary training facility, the Mechatronics Automation Lab, spans approximately 4,500 square feet within the College of Technical Sciences. The space is designed to mimic a modern industrial control environment, featuring modular workstations that allow for flexible configuration based on specific learning modules. The lab is equipped with six comprehensive mechatronics workbenches, each integrating mechanical assembly components, electrical wiring stations, and pneumatic/hydraulic training rigs. These workbenches serve as the primary interface for students to learn system integration, troubleshooting, and preventive maintenance.

Central to the lab’s instructional capacity are three programmable logic controller (PLC) trainers from leading manufacturers. These units allow students to progress from basic ladder logic programming to advanced motion control and data acquisition tasks. Each PLC trainer is paired with a human-machine interface (HMI) touchscreen panel, enabling students to visualize real-time system data and design user interfaces for industrial processes. Additionally, the lab houses five robotic arms ranging from 6-axis articulated models to collaborative robots (cobots). These units are used to teach programming, path planning, and safety protocols, ensuring that graduates are proficient in both traditional automation and emerging collaborative technologies.

## Sensor Kits and Diagnostic Tools

To support diagnostic and maintenance training, HCCC has invested heavily in a diverse inventory of sensor kits and diagnostic tools. The lab maintains three comprehensive sensor training stations, each equipped with a variety of industrial sensors including proximity switches, photoelectric cells, load cells, and temperature transducers. Students use these kits to calibrate sensors, interpret signal outputs, and troubleshoot circuit faults in simulated production environments.

Complementing the fixed equipment is a mobile diagnostic cart containing portable multimeters, oscilloscopes, thermal imaging cameras, and vibration analyzers. These tools allow instructors to conduct mobile assessments and enable students to practice field-service techniques in a controlled setting. The availability of such high-fidelity diagnostic equipment ensures that training aligns closely with the actual tools used by employers in the region, such as Kessler Fluid Systems and Tri-County Packaging Cooperative, both of which have expressed strong support for the SATP initiative through letters of commitment dated August 2026.

## Laboratory Space and Support Infrastructure

Beyond the specialized equipment, the lab space itself is designed to facilitate collaborative learning and safety compliance. The facility includes a dedicated instructor station with networked monitoring capabilities, allowing faculty to observe student progress on PLCs and robotic arms in real time. A separate clean room area is available for precision assembly tasks, while a materials storage zone ensures that components are organized and accessible for efficient project-based learning.

Safety is paramount in the lab’s design. All electrical stations are equipped with emergency stop circuits and grounding protocols. The robotic arm workstations feature physical guarding and light curtains to ensure safe human-robot interaction during training exercises. Furthermore, the lab includes a digital display wall that can be used for virtual reality (VR) simulations of hazardous industrial scenarios, providing students with risk-free exposure to complex troubleshooting situations.

## Alignment with SATP Goals

The existing facilities at HCCC are fully capable of supporting the ambitious goals of the Smart Automation Technician Pathway. With a target of 150 completers over three years, the current equipment inventory provides sufficient capacity for concurrent student cohorts. The integration of PLC trainers, robotic arms, and sensor kits directly addresses the skill gaps identified by regional employers, particularly in the areas of system integration and predictive maintenance. By building upon the legacy of the *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* project, SATP will not only utilize existing assets but also expand their utility through updated curriculum and enhanced industry partnerships. This continuity ensures that federal investment yields sustainable outcomes, preparing a new generation of technicians to meet the evolving demands of rural manufacturing industries in the Mid-South.
