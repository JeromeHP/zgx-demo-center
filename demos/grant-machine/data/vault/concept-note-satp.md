## Project Overview and Institutional Context

Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC), a public two-year institution serving four rural counties in the Mid-South, is uniquely positioned to address critical workforce gaps in advanced manufacturing and industrial automation. With a fall 2025 credit headcount of 6,840 students—of whom 52% are Pell Grant recipients and 41% are first-generation college students—HCCC demonstrates a deep commitment to equitable access and economic mobility for underserved populations. The proposed Smart Automation Technician Pathway (SATP) represents a strategic expansion of our existing technical education infrastructure, leveraging prior success in mechatronics training to meet the evolving demands of regional industry partners.

SATP is structured as an NSF Advanced Technology Educational (ATE) Track 2 project, focusing on curriculum development, implementation, and dissemination. Led by Principal Investigator Dr. Renee Okafor, Dean of Workforce and Technical Programs, and Co-Principal Investigator Marcus Lindqvist, Chair of Mechatronics and Industrial Automation, the project aims to produce 150 skilled automation technicians over a three-year period beginning July 1, 2027. This initiative builds directly upon the foundation laid by our previous NSF ATE award, *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* (HCCC-FICT-ATE-2021), which concluded in August 2025.

## Background and Preliminary Data

The success of SATP is grounded in empirical evidence from HCCC’s prior engagement with the National Science Foundation. The *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* project, funded at $598,112, ran from September 1, 2021, to August 31, 2025. While the project initially targeted 120 completers, it achieved 84 completions by the end of the award period. Despite falling short of the completion target due to supply chain delays in early 2023, the project demonstrated strong labor market alignment: 78% of the 61 respondents who provided placement data secured full-time employment in their field within six months of graduation.

The prior award resulted in the development of three new credit courses and established partnerships with 11 industry organizations, including two dual-credit agreements with local high schools. These outcomes validated the demand for automation-focused training in our service area and provided a robust network of employer stakeholders. SATP leverages these existing relationships to accelerate deployment and ensure immediate relevance to current technological shifts in manufacturing, specifically focusing on programmable logic controllers (PLCs), robotics integration, and predictive maintenance systems.

## Project Goals and Strategic Partnerships

The primary objective of SATP is to design, develop, and implement a comprehensive curriculum that prepares students for entry-level and mid-level roles in smart automation environments. The project targets 150 completers over three years, with an anticipated start date of July 1, 2027. To ensure curriculum rigor and industry alignment, SATP has secured formal letters of support from key regional employers who have identified a critical shortage of qualified technicians.

Notably, Kessler Fluid Systems submitted a letter of support dated August 12, 2026, explicitly referencing the SATP project and committing to provide internship placements for students. Similarly, Tri-County Packaging Cooperative provided a letter of support on August 20, 2026, highlighting their intent to hire graduates for roles involving automated packaging lines. These partnerships are complemented by the ongoing relationship with Northgate Precision Castings, which supported our prior mechatronics initiative and continues to serve as an advisory partner for SATP’s technical steering committee.

The project budget totals $1,038,450, reflecting the expanded scope of curriculum development, new equipment acquisition, and enhanced student support services. The budget adheres to HCCC’s institutional fiscal policies, applying a fringe rate of 28.0% as specified in the grant budget, while noting that the current faculty-fringe agreement (documented in *rate-agreement-fy2026*) operates at 31.5%. Indirect costs are calculated at 36.0% on Modified Total Direct Costs (MTDC), consistent with federal regulations and institutional policy.

## Implementation, Evaluation, and Dissemination

SATP will be implemented through a phased approach, beginning with curriculum mapping and equipment procurement in the first year, followed by pilot instruction and iterative refinement in the second year, and full-scale delivery in the third year. To ensure accountability and continuous improvement, the project engages Bluestem Evaluation Group, led by Dr. Priya Raman, as an external evaluator. Dr. Raman’s team will conduct mixed-methods assessments of student outcomes, employer satisfaction, and curriculum effectiveness, providing annual reports to guide program adjustments.

Dissemination activities will focus on sharing best practices with other community colleges in the Mid-South and beyond. Outputs include open-access instructional modules, faculty development workshops, and policy briefs on effective partnerships between educational institutions and rural manufacturers. By integrating the lessons learned from the prior *Mechatronics Pathways* project, SATP aims to achieve higher completion rates and stronger employment outcomes, thereby strengthening the regional economy and providing students with sustainable career pathways in high-growth technical sectors.
