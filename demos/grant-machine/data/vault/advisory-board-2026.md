## Minutes of the Industry Advisory Board Meeting

**Date:** August 15, 2026
**Time:** 9:00 AM – 11:30 AM CDT
**Location:** HCCC Campus Center, Conference Room B (Hybrid via Secure Video Link)
**Presiding:** Marcus Lindqvist, Chair, Mechatronics and Industrial Automation

### I. Call to Order and Welcome

Chair Lindqvist called the meeting to order at 9:05 AM. He welcomed the advisory board members and acknowledged the presence of Dr. Renee Okafor, Dean of Workforce and Technical Programs. The chair noted that this meeting is critical in the context of the upcoming National Science Foundation (NSF) Advanced Technological Education (ATE) proposal cycle, specifically for the proposed Smart Automation Technician Pathway (SATP).

### II. Review of Prior Award Outcomes: Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers

Dana Whitfield presented a summary of the outcomes from the prior NSF ATE award (HCCC-FICT-ATE-2021), titled *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers*. The award, active from September 1, 2021, to August 31, 2025, had a total budget of $598,112.

Key metrics reviewed included:
*   **Student Completion:** The project targeted 120 completers but achieved 84 actual completers. While this represents a shortfall in volume, the quality of outcomes was deemed strong.
*   **Placement Rate:** Of the 61 respondents tracked, 78% secured employment in relevant fields within six months of graduation. This high placement rate underscores the program’s relevance despite the lower completion numbers.
*   **Curriculum Development:** The award successfully launched three new courses focused on PLC programming and sensor integration.
*   **Partnerships:** The project established relationships with 11 industry partners, including current members present at this meeting such as Kessler Fluid Systems and Northgate Precision Castings. Additionally, two dual-credit agreements were secured with local high schools, a metric the board identified as a strength to expand upon.

Dr. Priya Raman of Bluestem Evaluation Group, serving as the external evaluator, provided context on the completion gap. She noted that while the target was not met, the retention strategies implemented in Year 3 showed improvement, suggesting that the proposed SATP’s enhanced support structures could help close this gap.

### III. Regional Labor Market Analysis and Skills Gap Discussion

Elias Thorne of Oakhaven Robotics Solutions led a discussion on the current regional labor market. He highlighted a significant and growing skills gap in automation maintenance and programming within the four-county service area. With the expansion of automated assembly lines in local manufacturing hubs, employers are struggling to find technicians who can troubleshoot integrated mechanical-electrical-software systems.

Sarah Jenkins from Central Plains Employment Services corroborated these findings with recent labor market data. She emphasized that entry-level automation roles are seeing a 15% year-over-year increase in postings, yet qualified applicants remain scarce. The board agreed that the existing curriculum, while effective, needs to be modernized to address Industry 4.0 standards, including IoT connectivity and predictive maintenance analytics.

### IV. Strategic Direction: Proposal for NSF ATE Funding (SATP)

Marcus Lindqvist introduced the primary agenda item: the pursuit of new NSF ATE funding for the *Smart Automation Technician Pathway* (SATP). The proposed project is a three-year initiative (July 1, 2027 – June 30, 2030) with a total budget request of $1,038,450.

The board discussed the strategic rationale for SATP:
1.  **Addressing the Skills Gap:** The program aims to train 150 completers over three years, directly targeting the shortage identified by local employers.
2.  **Enhanced Support Services:** Leveraging lessons from the prior award, SATP will incorporate more robust advising and peer-mentoring components to improve completion rates.
3.  **Industry Alignment:** The curriculum will be co-designed with partners like Kessler Fluid Systems and Tri-County Packaging Cooperative to ensure immediate applicability.

Dr. Okafor emphasized that HCCC’s mission as a public two-year college serving a rural Mid-South region makes this funding critical for economic mobility. With 52% of students being Pell recipients and 41% first-generation college students, the SATP model prioritizes accessible, high-quality technical education.

### V. Letter of Support and Employer Commitment

Dana Whitfield reported that three key regional employers have submitted letters of support for the SATP proposal:
*   **Kessler Fluid Systems** (Letter dated August 12, 2026) committed to providing internship slots and equipment donations.
*   **Tri-County Packaging Cooperative** (Letter dated August 20, 2026) pledged to participate in curriculum review panels and hire graduates.
*   **Northgate Precision Castings** (Letter dated March 15, 2021, renewed for current cycle) reaffirmed their long-standing partnership and interest in hiring SATP graduates.

The board noted that these commitments demonstrate strong industry buy-in, a critical component for NSF ATE review panels.
