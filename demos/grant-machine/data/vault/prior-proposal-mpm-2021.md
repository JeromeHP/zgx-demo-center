## Project Overview and Strategic Alignment

Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC), a public two-year institution serving four rural counties in the Mid-South, is pleased to submit this excerpt from our funded National Science Foundation Advanced Technological Education (NSF ATE) proposal titled "Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers." Awarded under identifier HCCC-FICT-ATE-2021 with total funding of $598,112, this project operates within a critical window designed to address the growing skills gap in rural industrial manufacturing. The project period commenced on September 1, 2021, and is scheduled to conclude on August 31, 2025, including a one-year no-cost extension granted to ensure comprehensive evaluation and dissemination of results.

As an institution with a fall 2025 credit headcount of 6,840 students, HCCC maintains a deep commitment to accessibility and workforce development. With 52% of our student body receiving Pell Grants and 41% identifying as first-generation college students, our mission is intrinsically linked to economic mobility for underserved populations. The "Mechatronics Pathways" initiative represents a strategic response to the specific needs of our service area, where traditional manufacturing is evolving rapidly into smart, automated production environments. By aligning our academic offerings with the real-time demands of local industry, this project seeks to create a sustainable pipeline of skilled technicians capable of operating, maintaining, and troubleshooting advanced mechatronic systems.

## Programmatic Activities and Infrastructure Development

The core of the "Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers" project is built upon three interconnected pillars: curriculum development, industry partnership expansion, and laboratory infrastructure modernization. These activities are designed to work synergistically to create a holistic educational experience that bridges the gap between classroom theory and shop-floor reality.

### Curriculum Innovation and New Coursework

A primary objective of this grant is to update and expand our mechatronics curriculum to reflect current industry standards. The project has facilitated the development of three new specialized courses that integrate mechanical, electrical, and computer engineering principles. These courses are not merely theoretical; they are designed around authentic problem-solving scenarios drawn directly from partner employers. By embedding real-world automation challenges into the coursework, students gain practical competencies that are immediately applicable upon graduation. The curriculum revision process has involved continuous feedback loops with industry advisory boards to ensure relevance and rigor.

### Employer Partnerships and Industry Engagement

Recognizing that workforce development cannot occur in isolation from industry, HCCC has prioritized the establishment of strong employer partnerships. Through this project, we have formalized relationships with 11 industry partners across our four-county service area. These partnerships extend beyond simple advisory roles; they involve active collaboration in curriculum design, guest lecturing, internship provision, and hiring commitments.

The engagement strategy includes the creation of dual-credit agreements to introduce high school students to mechatronics concepts earlier in their academic journeys. Currently, HCCC has established two such dual-credit agreements, allowing interested secondary students to earn college credits while still in high school. This early exposure helps build a stronger pipeline of talent for our program and addresses the "brain drain" often experienced in rural communities by encouraging local youth to pursue technical careers at home.

### Industrial Automation Laboratory Upgrades

To support the new curriculum and provide hands-on training, significant investments have been made in upgrading our industrial automation laboratory. These upgrades include the acquisition of modern programmable logic controllers (PLCs), robotic arms, sensor arrays, and human-machine interface (HMI) systems. The lab environment is designed to mimic a real-world manufacturing floor, complete with safety protocols and production line simulations. This state-of-the-art facility serves as a crucible for student learning, allowing them to develop the tactile and diagnostic skills that are difficult to teach through traditional lecture-based instruction alone.

## Project Outcomes and Impact Assessment

The "Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers" project has set clear, measurable targets to evaluate its success. The primary outcome metric is the number of students who complete the mechatronics pathway program. Over the course of the project period (2021-2025), HCCC targeted the completion of 120 students. This target reflects a balanced approach to capacity building, ensuring that the program remains manageable and high-quality while making a significant impact on the local workforce.

As of the most recent data collection points prior to the project's conclusion, the program has achieved notable success in meeting its enrollment and completion goals. The actual number of completers reached 84 students. While this figure represents strong progress, it also highlights areas for continued improvement in student retention and support services. Of those 84 completers, data indicates that 61 respondents provided detailed employment information, allowing for a precise assessment of placement rates.

The employment outcomes for graduates have been particularly promising. The project has achieved a placement rate of 78% among the responding graduates. This high rate underscores the strong demand for mechatronics technicians in the rural Mid-South region and validates the effectiveness of HCCC’s industry-aligned curriculum. Employers in the region have reported that HCCC graduates are well-prepared, technically proficient, and ready to contribute to their operations from day one.

## Conclusion and Future Directions

The "Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers" project has successfully demonstrated that community colleges can play a pivotal role in addressing the skilled labor shortage in rural manufacturing sectors. By leveraging federal funding through the NSF ATE program, HCCC has been able to modernize its facilities, update its curriculum, and forge deep partnerships with local industry. The achievements of 84 completers and a 78% placement rate serve as a testament to the dedication of our faculty, staff, and industry partners.
