## Strategic Priority: Advanced Manufacturing Automation

Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC) has identified advanced manufacturing automation as a named strategic priority within its 2025-2030 Strategic Plan. This designation is not merely aspirational; it is a direct response to urgent, documented demand from regional employers across the four rural counties in the Mid-South that constitute our service area. As local industries undergo rapid digital transformation, the gap between available technical talent and the complex skills required to operate, maintain, and optimize automated systems has widened significantly. HCCC is uniquely positioned to bridge this gap by expanding technician training pathways, building directly upon the foundational success of its existing Mechatronics and Industrial Automation program.

The decision to elevate automation to a strategic priority is driven by empirical data from our recent workforce outcomes and ongoing dialogue with industry stakeholders. Our service area, characterized by a high proportion of first-generation students (41%) and Pell Grant recipients (52%), represents an untapped reservoir of potential talent. However, traditional entry-level manufacturing roles are increasingly being automated, leaving workers vulnerable to displacement without upskilling. By focusing on advanced automation, HCCC ensures that our graduates are not just participants in the modern workforce but essential operators of its technological infrastructure. This aligns with our institutional mission to provide accessible, high-impact education that drives regional economic resilience.

## Building on Proven Foundations: The Mechatronics and Industrial Automation Program

The strategic expansion into advanced automation is rooted in the proven efficacy of HCCC’s existing Mechatronics and Industrial Automation program, chaired by Marcus Lindqvist. This program has served as the primary pipeline for technical talent in the region for the past five years. Its success was significantly bolstered by the NSF ATE-funded project "Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers" (Award ID: HCCC-FICT-ATE-2021), which ran from September 1, 2021, to August 31, 2025.

While the prior award achieved a placement rate of 78% among respondents and established partnerships with 11 industry partners, it also highlighted critical areas for improvement, particularly in completion rates. The project targeted 120 completers but saw 84 actual completions, indicating that while demand was high, structural barriers prevented nearly one-third of enrolled students from finishing their credentials. Furthermore, the introduction of three new courses and two dual-credit agreements demonstrated the program’s agility in curriculum development but also revealed the need for more robust student support systems and clearer pathway articulation.

Under Dr. Renee Okafor’s leadership as Dean of Workforce and Technical Programs, HCCC has analyzed these outcomes to refine its approach. The lesson from the prior award is clear: while interest in automation training is strong, retention and completion require a more structured, supported, and industry-aligned pathway. The new strategic initiative will leverage the instructional framework established by Chair Lindqvist but will introduce enhanced advising, modular credentialing, and deeper integration with employer-specific automation standards to address the completion gap observed in the previous cycle.

## Employer-Driven Curriculum and Regional Demand

The curriculum for the upcoming Smart Automation Technician Pathway (SATP) is not developed in isolation; it is co-created with regional employers who have explicitly signaled their need for this specific skill set. Three major local manufacturers—Kessler Fluid Systems, Tri-County Packaging Cooperative, and Northgate Precision Castings—have provided formal letters of support referencing the SATP project, underscoring the immediacy of this workforce need.

Kessler Fluid Systems, in a letter dated August 12, 2026, emphasized the urgent need for technicians capable of troubleshooting PLC-based fluid control systems, a niche skill set that current community college offerings do not adequately cover. Similarly, Tri-County Packaging Cooperative highlighted their transition to fully automated packaging lines on August 20, 2026, noting a critical shortage of personnel who can interface between mechanical maintenance and software diagnostics. Northgate Precision Castings, while referencing an earlier partnership with the Mechatronics Pathways project, has reiterated its commitment to hiring HCCC graduates for its newly installed robotic casting cells.

These employer engagements confirm that the regional economy is not waiting for a future state of automation; it is already here. The SATP is designed to respond directly to these specific operational challenges, ensuring that every hour of training translates to immediate workplace applicability. By aligning our instructional objectives with the real-time needs of Kessler, Tri-County, and Northgate, HCCC ensures that its graduates are job-ready from day one, thereby increasing placement rates and reducing the time-to-hire for employers.

## Commitment to Accessible, High-Impact Workforce Development

Harrow Creek Community College remains committed to serving a diverse student population in a rural region often overlooked by technical training initiatives. With a credit headcount of 6,840 as of Fall 2025, HCCC understands that workforce development must be accessible to those who need it most. The SATP initiative will prioritize recruitment among first-generation students and low-income learners, ensuring that the benefits of advanced manufacturing automation are not limited to those with prior technical education or financial means.

This strategic plan excerpt outlines a clear trajectory: leveraging past successes, learning from previous limitations, and responding directly to employer demand. By expanding technician training pathways under the leadership of Dr. Okafor and Chair Lindqvist, HCCC is poised to transform the regional labor market.
