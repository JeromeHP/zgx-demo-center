## Institutional Overview and Leadership

Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC) is a public two-year institution dedicated to serving four rural counties in the Mid-South. As an anchor institution within its service area, HCCC plays a pivotal role in fostering educational attainment, workforce development, and economic resilience for a region characterized by both agricultural heritage and emerging industrial sectors. Under the leadership of President Dr. Alan Pruett, the college has maintained a steadfast commitment to accessibility, academic excellence, and community engagement. The institution operates with a clear mandate to provide high-quality postsecondary education that is responsive to the evolving needs of local employers and students alike.

For the fall 2025 term, HCCC reported a credit headcount of 6,840 students. This enrollment figure reflects the college’s sustained relevance in a competitive higher education landscape. The student body at HCCC is defined by its diversity and socioeconomic background, with 52% of students identified as Pell Grant recipients. Furthermore, 41% of the student population comprises first-generation college students, underscoring the institution’s critical function as a gateway to upward mobility for families who have historically lacked access to higher education. These demographics highlight the urgent need for robust support systems and career-aligned programming that can bridge the gap between educational completion and meaningful employment.

## Academic Programs and Workforce Development

HCCC offers a comprehensive suite of academic programs designed to meet both transfer and workforce preparation goals. The curriculum spans liberal arts, sciences, health professions, and technical trades, ensuring that students have multiple pathways to achieve their personal and professional objectives. Central to the college’s strategic mission is its robust workforce and technical education division, which partners closely with regional industries to ensure that training programs remain current and relevant.

The Dean of Workforce and Technical Programs, Dr. Renee Okafor, oversees a curriculum that emphasizes hands-on learning, industry certifications, and seamless integration with local economic demands. Within this framework, the Mechatronics and Industrial Automation program, chaired by Marcus Lindqvist, has emerged as a flagship initiative. This program addresses the growing demand for skilled technicians capable of operating and maintaining advanced automated systems in manufacturing environments. By aligning course content with industry standards, HCCC ensures that graduates are not only employable but also prepared to lead technological advancements within their respective fields.

The college’s approach to workforce development is deeply collaborative. Advisory boards composed of local employers, community leaders, and educational experts regularly review program outcomes and curriculum relevance. This iterative process allows HCCC to adapt quickly to shifts in labor market trends, ensuring that its offerings remain aligned with the needs of regional stakeholders. The institution’s commitment to continuous improvement is evident in its willingness to innovate, as demonstrated by its history of successful grant-funded initiatives that have expanded access and improved outcomes for students.

## Role as an Anchor Institution

As an anchor institution, HCCC serves as a vital economic engine for the four rural counties it serves. The college maintains strong partnerships with major regional employers, including Kessler Fluid Systems, Tri-County Packaging Cooperative, and Northgate Precision Castings. These relationships are formalized through letters of support and active participation in curriculum development, ensuring that training programs produce graduates who possess the specific skills required by local industries.

The collaboration between HCCC and these employers extends beyond mere job placement. It involves a shared investment in workforce readiness, where companies provide input on emerging technologies and safety standards, while the college provides the educational infrastructure to train the next generation of technicians. For instance, Kessler Fluid Systems has expressed strong support for the upcoming Smart Automation Technician Pathway (SATP), recognizing the need for skilled labor to support their expansion plans. Similarly, Tri-County Packaging Cooperative has indicated its intent to partner with HCCC on internship opportunities and direct hiring pipelines, reinforcing the college’s role in sustaining local employment.

This anchor institution model is further strengthened by HCCC’s participation in prior federal funding initiatives. The college successfully completed a National Science Foundation Advanced Technological Education (ATE) award titled "Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers" (Award ID: HCCC-FICT-ATE-2021). Running from September 1, 2021, to August 31, 2025, this project provided valuable insights into the challenges and opportunities of rural workforce development. The outcomes of this prior award demonstrated the effectiveness of targeted interventions in increasing student completion and placement rates, providing a strong foundation for future proposals.
