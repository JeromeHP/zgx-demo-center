## Panel Review Summary: Smart Automation Technician Pathway

**Proposal Title:** Smart Automation Technician Pathway (SATP)
**Institution:** Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC)
**Program Officer:** [Reserved for NSF Assignment]
**Review Date:** September 16, 2026

### Executive Overview

The review panel convened to evaluate the proposal submitted by Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC) for funding under the National Science Foundation’s Advanced Technological Education (ATE) program. The proposed project, titled "Smart Automation Technician Pathway" (SATP), seeks to develop and implement a new workforce training track designed to address critical skill gaps in rural manufacturing sectors across HCCC’s service area of four Mid-South counties.

The panel conducted a thorough assessment of the proposal’s alignment with NSF ATE priorities, the capacity of the institutional team, the rigor of the project design, and the adequacy of the evaluation plan. The proposal demonstrates significant potential to enhance technical education infrastructure in underrepresented rural regions. However, specific concerns regarding the metrics of success and the specificity of the assessment methodology require attention prior to final award determination.

### Strengths of the Proposal

The panel identified several compelling strengths that support the viability and impact of the SATP initiative. Foremost among these is the robust evidence of employer engagement and commitment. The proposal includes strong, detailed letters of support from key regional industry partners, including Kessler Fluid Systems, Tri-County Packaging Cooperative, and Northgate Precision Castings. These documents confirm a clear demand for smart automation skills within the local manufacturing economy and outline specific commitments to internship placements, equipment access, and curriculum advisory roles. The dates of these letters (August 2026) indicate current and active collaboration, reinforcing the project’s relevance to immediate regional needs.

Additionally, the Principal Investigator team presents a highly experienced leadership structure capable of executing complex technical training initiatives. Dr. Renee Okafor, serving as Principal Investigator and Dean of Workforce and Technical Programs at HCCC, brings substantial administrative authority and strategic oversight. Co-Principal Investigator Marcus Lindqvist, Chair of Mechatronics and Industrial Automation, provides the necessary technical expertise to ensure curriculum accuracy and industry alignment. This combination of administrative leadership and subject-matter expertise positions the team well to navigate both institutional requirements and pedagogical challenges.

The panel also noted HCCC’s strong commitment to serving diverse student populations. With a Pell Grant recipient population of 52% and 41% first-generation students, the college is uniquely positioned to expand access to high-tech manufacturing careers for underrepresented groups. The proposed budget, totaling $1,038,450 over three years, reflects a realistic scope for developing new instructional materials, securing industry partnerships, and supporting student recruitment and retention activities.

### Weaknesses and Concerns

Despite the strong foundation, the panel raised significant concerns regarding the project’s evaluation framework and outcome targets. The primary weakness identified is that the evaluation plan was underspecified. While the proposal acknowledges the involvement of Bluestem Evaluation Group and Dr. Priya Raman as lead evaluator, the narrative lacks detailed methodological descriptions. Specifically, the proposal does not sufficiently articulate how data will be collected to measure changes in student self-efficacy, technical competency gains, or long-term career progression. The panel emphasized that ATE proposals require a rigorous, mixed-methods evaluation design that clearly links project activities to intended outcomes. Without a more detailed evaluation plan, it is difficult to assess whether the project’s success can be reliably measured and reported to the NSF.

Furthermore, the panel expressed concern regarding the ambition of the completer targets relative to regional enrollment trends. The proposal sets a target of 150 completers over the three-year project period. Given HCCC’s current fall 2025 credit headcount of 6,840 students and the specific niche nature of smart automation training, the panel questioned whether this target is achievable without a significantly expanded recruitment strategy or broader industry partnerships. The panel noted that while previous ATE awards at HCCC showed promise, actual completers (84 out of a target of 120) fell short of projections in prior cycles. This historical performance data, combined with the specialized nature of the SATP track, suggests that the new target may be overly optimistic without a more granular breakdown of recruitment pathways and conversion rates.

### Panel Recommendation

The panel recommends funding for the Smart Automation Technician Pathway proposal, contingent upon the submission of a revised evaluation plan that addresses the identified weaknesses. The project’s strong employer support, experienced leadership team, and alignment with regional economic needs make it a worthy candidate for ATE investment. However, to ensure accountability and measurable impact, HCCC must provide a comprehensive evaluation framework that details data collection instruments, analysis methods, and realistic milestones for student completion.

It is recommended that the project director work closely with Dr. Priya Raman of Bluestem Evaluation Group to develop a robust assessment strategy that includes both formative and summative measures. Additionally, the panel suggests revisiting the completer targets to ensure they are grounded in achievable recruitment metrics, possibly by breaking down the 150-completer goal into annual increments tied to specific industry partnership activations.

With these revisions, HCCC is well-positioned to deliver a high-quality educational experience that strengthens the smart automation workforce in rural Mid-South communities. The panel looks forward to reviewing the revised evaluation plan and supporting the successful implementation of this vital initiative.
