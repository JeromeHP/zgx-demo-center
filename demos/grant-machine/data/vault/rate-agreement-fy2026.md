## Purpose and Scope

This memorandum serves as the official notification of negotiated indirect cost and fringe benefit rates for Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC) for Fiscal Year 2026. Issued by the Office of Grants and Sponsored Programs, this document establishes the financial parameters applicable to all sponsored projects budgeted within FY2026. The rates outlined herein are binding for new proposals submitted after the effective date and must be applied to existing active awards where FY2026 costs are incurred.

The primary objective of this memo is to ensure compliance with federal and state regulations governing sponsored research and workforce development grants. By standardizing these calculations, HCCC aims to maintain fiscal integrity while maximizing the net resources available for direct project activities. All Principal Investigators (PIs), Co-Principal Investigators (Co-PIs), and administrative staff must utilize these rates when preparing budgets, justifying costs, and managing financial reports for the upcoming fiscal period.

## Negotiated Indirect Cost Rate

HCCC has secured a negotiated indirect cost rate of **36.0%** of Modified Total Direct Costs (MTDC) for FY2026. This rate reflects the college’s commitment to recovering a fair share of institutional overhead expenses, including facilities maintenance, utilities, library resources, and administrative support services that facilitate the successful execution of sponsored projects.

The base for this calculation remains MTDC, which is defined as the sum of all direct salaries and fringe benefits, materials, supplies, travel, and subcontract costs up to the first $25,000 of each subaward. Items such as equipment, capital expenditures, and the portion of each subaward exceeding $25,000 are excluded from the MTDC base in accordance with standard federal cost principles.

For example, a new proposal for the *Smart Automation Technician Pathway (SATP)*, which carries a total budget request of $1,038,450 over three years, must allocate indirect costs based on this 36.0% rate applied to its MTDC base. This ensures that the institution’s overhead contribution is accurately reflected in the final negotiated award amount, preventing under-recovery of essential operational costs.

## Negotiated Fringe Benefit Rate

The approved fringe benefit rate for FY2026 is **31.5%** of gross salaries and wages. This percentage encompasses all employer-paid benefits associated with employee compensation, including but not limited to:

*   Social Security (FICA)
*   Medicare
*   Workers’ Compensation Insurance
*   Health Insurance Contributions
*   Retirement Plan Contributions
*   Paid Leave (Vacation, Sick, Holiday)

It is critical for budget developers to distinguish between the fringe rate used in prior proposals and the current negotiated rate. While previous budget templates may have utilized a lower estimate (such as 28.0%), the FY2026 memo mandates the use of the confirmed 31.5% rate. Failure to apply this updated rate will result in budget inaccuracies, potential compliance issues during financial reviews, and possible disallowance of costs by sponsors.

For instance, if a faculty member’s salary is allocated $50,000 toward a project, the corresponding fringe benefit cost must be calculated as $15,750 ($50,000 x 0.315). This calculation must be applied consistently across all personnel categories, from instructional staff to administrative support, ensuring equitable treatment of all budget lines.

## Implementation and Compliance

These rates are effective immediately for FY2026 and remain in force until superseded by a new negotiated agreement. All new proposals submitted to funding agencies, including those targeting workforce development tracks like the *Smart Automation Technician Pathway*, must incorporate these figures into their line-item budgets.

The Office of Grants and Sponsored Programs, led by Director Dana Whitfield, will conduct pre-award reviews to verify the correct application of these rates. Any discrepancies identified during the review process will require revision before submission. Furthermore, institutions with existing active awards, such as the *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* project supported by NSF ATE, should consult with their financial officers regarding carryover funds and whether new indirect costs apply to unexpended balances incurred in FY2026.

Adherence to these rates is not optional; it is a condition of HCCC’s continued eligibility for sponsored funding. By maintaining accurate and compliant budgets, we protect the institution’s reputation and ensure sustainable support for our faculty, students, and community partners across the four rural counties of the Mid-South service area.
