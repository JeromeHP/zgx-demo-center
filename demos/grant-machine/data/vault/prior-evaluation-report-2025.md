## Executive Summary

This report presents the external evaluation findings for the National Science Foundation Advanced Technological Education (NSF ATE) project titled *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* (Award ID: HCCC-FICT-ATE-2021), administered by Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC). Conducted by Bluestem Evaluation Group, this assessment covers the full five-year period from September 1, 2021, through August 31, 2025. The primary objective of this evaluation was to determine the extent to which the project achieved its intended outcomes regarding student completion, workforce placement, and curriculum development, while identifying systemic factors that influenced performance relative to established targets.

The evaluation indicates that the project successfully launched three new courses, secured eleven industry partnerships, and established two dual-credit agreements with local high schools. However, the project fell short of its primary student completion target, reporting 84 completers against a goal of 120. Despite this shortfall, the program demonstrated strong labor market relevance, with a 78% placement rate among the 61 respondents who provided post-completion data. This report attributes the enrollment and completion gaps to exogenous factors, specifically pandemic-era demographic shifts and a critical instructor vacancy, rather than deficiencies in curriculum design or pedagogical approach. Based on these findings, Bluestem Evaluation Group recommends specific structural improvements for the proposed follow-on project, *Smart Automation Technician Pathway* (SATP), focusing on enhanced early-alert advising, diversified employer engagement, and the integration of formal formative evaluation cycles.

## Project Overview and Context

Harrow Creek Community College is a public two-year institution serving four rural counties in the fictional Mid-South region. With a fall 2025 credit headcount of 6,840 students, HCCC serves a diverse population, including 52% Pell Grant recipients and 41% first-generation college students. The *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* project was designed to address the growing demand for skilled technicians in the region’s manufacturing sector by aligning technical education with industry needs.

Led by Principal Investigator Dr. Renee Okafor, Dean of Workforce and Technical Programs, and Co-Principal Investigator Marcus Lindqvist, Chair of Mechatronics and Industrial Automation, the project operated under a budget of $598,112. The initiative aimed to modernize the mechatronics curriculum, strengthen industry partnerships, and increase the pipeline of certified technicians for local employers. Dr. Dana Whitfield, Director of Grants and Sponsored Programs, provided administrative oversight to ensure compliance with NSF requirements throughout the award period.

## Performance Against Targets

The evaluation framework utilized a mixed-methods approach, analyzing quantitative completion data, employment outcomes, and qualitative feedback from stakeholders. The project’s primary metric for success was the number of students completing the mechatronics certificate or degree pathway within the five-year window.

### Student Completion Rates
The project established a target of 120 completers. By the close of the award period on August 31, 2025, HCCC had documented 84 completers. This represents 70% of the projected goal. While the absolute number of graduates indicates a robust program output, the variance from the target requires contextual analysis. The evaluation team identified two primary drivers for this discrepancy:

1.  **Pandemic-Era Enrollment Declines:** The project launch coincided with significant disruptions in higher education enrollment due to the ongoing impacts of the global pandemic. HCCC experienced a broader institutional decline in first-year retention and new student intake, particularly among the demographic most likely to enter technical programs. This macro-environmental factor reduced the available applicant pool for the mechatronics pathway during the critical recruitment phases of Years 1 through 3.
2.  **Instructor Vacancy:** Between Year 2 and Year 3, the program experienced an unplanned vacancy in the lead instructor position. Although HCCC eventually filled the role, the transition period resulted in a temporary reduction in course offerings and a delay in the onboarding of new students into the advanced sequencing tracks. This bottleneck compressed the throughput of students who entered the program during that window.

### Workforce Placement Outcomes
Among the 84 completers, 61 respondents provided verified employment data. Of these, 78% secured positions within six months of graduation in roles directly related to mechatronics, industrial automation, or advanced manufacturing. This placement rate exceeds regional averages for similar technical programs and validates the relevance of the curriculum to local industry needs. The success in placement suggests that while enrollment challenges affected volume, the quality of training remained high and aligned with employer expectations.

### Curriculum and Partnership Development
Beyond student outcomes, the project achieved significant structural milestones:
*   **Curriculum Innovation:** Three new courses were developed and implemented, integrating modern automation technologies into the existing catalog.
*   **Industry Engagement:** The project established formal partnerships with eleven industry organizations. These partnerships facilitated guest lectures, equipment donations, and internship opportunities.
*   **Dual Credit Expansion:** Two dual-credit agreements were signed with local high schools, creating an earlier pipeline for interested students.

## Critical Analysis of Challenges

While the placement rate is a positive indicator, the shortfall in completers highlights vulnerabilities in the project’s operational resilience. The evaluation identifies that the program lacked sufficient buffer mechanisms to withstand external shocks such as staff turnover or broader enrollment fluctuations. Furthermore, the reliance on a limited number of key personnel created a single point of failure during the instructor vacancy period.

The industry partnerships, while numerous, varied in depth of engagement. While eleven letters of support and commitment were documented from partners such as Northgate Precision Castings (referenced in project materials dated March 15, 2021), the intensity of collaboration differed. Some partners provided advisory input, while others offered tangible resources like equipment or dedicated internship slots. This variability suggests an opportunity to standardize partnership expectations and deepen engagement models in future iterations.

## Recommendations for Follow-On Project: Smart Automation Technician Pathway (SATP)

Bluestem Evaluation Group recommends that the proposed *Smart Automation Technician Pathway* (SATP), scheduled to begin on July 1, 2027, with a target of 150 completers and a budget of $1,038,450, incorporate the following strategic improvements derived from the lessons learned during the prior award cycle.

### 1. Strengthen Early-Alert Advising Systems
To mitigate the risk of attrition that contributed to the completion shortfall in the previous project, SATP must implement a robust early-alert system. Given HCCC’s high percentage of first-generation and Pell-eligible students, proactive advising is essential. The evaluation recommends:
*   **Integrated Data Tracking:** Utilize student information systems to identify at-risk students based on academic performance, attendance, and financial aid status in the first eight weeks of each term.
*   **Dedicated Advising Staff:** Allocate resources within the SATP budget to hire or assign dedicated academic advisors who specialize in technical pathways. This role should focus on non-academic barriers such as childcare, transportation, and technology access, which are prevalent in rural service areas.
*   **Mentorship Programs:** Establish a peer-mentorship network connecting current students with recent graduates and industry partners to foster community and retention.

### 2. Diversify and Deepen Employer Partnerships
While the previous project secured eleven partners, SATP should aim for higher-quality engagement rather than sheer quantity. The evaluation notes that employers such as Kessler Fluid Systems (Letter dated August 12, 2026) and Tri-County Packaging Cooperative (Letter dated August 20, 2026) have already expressed interest in the new pathway. Recommendations include:
*   **Structured Advisory Board:** Formalize an industry advisory board with clear charters outlining roles, responsibilities, and expected contributions (e.g., curriculum review, equipment donation, internship provision).
*   **Tiered Partnership Models:** Create tiers of engagement (e.g., Platinum, Gold, Silver) that incentivize employers to provide deeper levels of support, such as co-developing course modules or funding specialized labs.
*   **Regional Expansion:** Leverage HCCC’s service area across four rural counties to broaden the employer base, reducing dependency on a single local economic sector and enhancing student placement options.

### 3. Implement Formal Formative Evaluation Cycles
The previous project relied heavily on summative evaluation at the end of the award period, which limited the ability to make mid-course corrections. SATP must embed formative evaluation from Year One. This includes:
*   **Annual Review Cycles:** Conduct annual reviews of program data (enrollment, retention, placement) with the advisory board and internal stakeholders to identify emerging trends or bottlenecks.
*   **Student Feedback Loops:** Implement regular surveys and focus groups with current students to assess curriculum relevance, instructional quality, and support service effectiveness.
*   **Adaptive Management:** Establish a protocol for using formative data to adjust program strategies in real-time. For example, if early-alert data shows a spike in attrition in specific courses, immediate curricular or pedagogical interventions can be deployed rather than waiting for the end-of-award report.

## Conclusion

The *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* project demonstrated significant success in curriculum development and workforce placement, despite challenges related to external enrollment declines and staffing disruptions. The 78% placement rate among respondents underscores the value of the training provided by HCCC’s Workforce and Technical Programs. By addressing the identified vulnerabilities through strengthened advising, deeper industry collaboration, and continuous formative evaluation, the proposed *Smart Automation Technician Pathway* is well-positioned to exceed its ambitious targets and further solidify Harrow Creek Community College’s role as a leader in technical education for the Mid-South region.
