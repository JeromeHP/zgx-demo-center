## Executive Summary and Funding Decision

This document serves as an internal retrospective and administrative record regarding the U.S. Department of Labor (DOL) Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA) Title I-B Adult Dislocated Worker Formula Grant application submitted by Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC) in early 2023. The proposal, tentatively titled *Advanced Manufacturing Skills Initiative*, sought funding to expand technical training capacities for the four rural counties comprising HCCC’s service area in the Mid-South.

Despite the rigorous development process led by Principal Investigator Dr. Renee Okafor, Dean of Workforce and Technical Programs, and Co-Principal Investigator Marcus Lindqvist, Chair of Mechatronics and Industrial Automation, the proposal was not funded. The Department of Labor review panel determined that while the instructional design was sound, the proposal lacked sufficient evidence of employer commitment and a robust plan for long-term financial sustainability post-grant. This decision, though disappointing, provided critical intelligence that directly informed the structural improvements made in subsequent grant submissions, most notably the 2026 Smart Automation Technician Pathway (SATP).

## Proposal Context and Strategic Alignment

The 2023 application emerged from a period of strategic assessment following the successful execution of HCCC’s prior National Science Foundation Advanced Technological Education (NSF ATE) award, *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* (HCCC-FICT-ATE-2021). That prior award, which ran from September 1, 2021, to August 31, 2025, provided a foundational framework for technical instruction. Although the ATE grant fell short of its completers target (84 actual vs. 120 targeted) and saw a placement rate of 78% among 61 respondents, it successfully established three new courses and secured dual credit agreements with two local high schools. The initiative engaged eleven industry partners, demonstrating HCCC’s capacity to deliver specialized manufacturing training.

Recognizing the need for broader workforce development funding beyond NSF limits, HCCC targeted DOL WIOA funds in 2023. The proposed *Advanced Manufacturing Skills Initiative* aimed to leverage the existing infrastructure from the ATE grant to serve a larger cohort of adult learners and dislocated workers. Dana Whitfield, Director of Grants and Sponsored Programs, coordinated the submission, ensuring alignment with federal priorities for rural economic revitalization. Given that HCCC serves a population where 52% of students are Pell Grant recipients and 41% are first-generation college students, the proposal emphasized accessibility and career ladder progression in advanced manufacturing sectors.

## Reviewer Feedback and Deficiencies

The DOL review panel provided detailed comments outlining the reasons for non-funding. The primary critique centered on the weakness of employer engagement strategies. While HCCC submitted a letter of support from Northgate Precision Castings, dated March 15, 2021, reviewers noted that this document was over two years old and referenced a previous NSF initiative rather than the proposed DOL project. Consequently, the panel judged the employer engagement as "thin," lacking current, specific commitments to hire graduates or contribute in-kind resources for the new program. The single letter of support was deemed insufficient to demonstrate the strong labor market demand required for DOL funding, particularly given the rural nature of HCCC’s service area.

A secondary deficiency identified by reviewers was the sustainability plan. The proposal outlined a budget reliant heavily on federal funds without a clear mechanism for institutional or employer cost-sharing after the grant period ended. Reviewers expressed concern that without guaranteed employer hiring commitments or dedicated local funding streams, the training infrastructure would likely atrophy once the DOL funds expired. Dr. Priya Raman of Bluestem Evaluation Group, who had previously evaluated HCCC’s NSF outcomes, noted in her advisory capacity that the lack of a diversified funding model posed a significant risk to program longevity.

## Institutional Response and Strategic Pivot

HCCC administration, led by President Dr. Alan Pruett and the grants leadership team, accepted the reviewer feedback as a constructive roadmap for improvement. The decision not to fund the 2023 proposal was treated not as a failure of technical competence, but as a validation of the need for deeper community integration. HCCC recognized that for rural workforce development to succeed, it must be co-created with employers rather than simply offered to them.

In response, HCCC initiated a comprehensive employer outreach campaign in 2024 and 2025. This effort shifted from soliciting generic letters of support to negotiating specific Memoranda of Understanding (MOUs) that included hiring commitments, equipment donations, and curriculum advisory roles. The college also revised its financial planning to incorporate indirect cost recovery and fringe benefit rates more accurately, moving toward a budget model that reflected the actual 31.5% fringe rate agreement documented in *rate-agreement-fy2026* and a 36.0% indirect rate on Modified Total Direct Costs (MTDC).

## Legacy and Application to Current Initiatives

The lessons learned from the declined 2023 DOL proposal directly shaped the design of the current Smart Automation Technician Pathway (SATP), launched in July 2027. Unlike the 2023 application, SATP features three robust letters of support from key regional employers: Kessler Fluid Systems (letter dated August 12, 2026), Tri-County Packaging Cooperative (letter dated August 20, 2026), and Northgate Precision Castings. These documents are current, specific to the SATP project, and outline tangible commitments to hiring graduates.

Furthermore, the sustainability plan for SATP is significantly stronger, leveraging the institutional stability of HCCC as a public two-year college with a fall 2025 credit headcount of 6,840. The budget for SATP, totaling $1,038,450 over three years, includes detailed projections for fringe benefits at a budgeted rate of 28.0%, acknowledging the gap between actual agreement rates and budgeted estimates to ensure financial realism.

By addressing the specific deficiencies identified in the 2023 review—namely, thin employer engagement and weak sustainability planning—HCCC has positioned SATP as a more resilient and community-integrated initiative. This iterative approach to grant writing demonstrates HCCC’s commitment to continuous improvement and its ability to translate feedback into actionable programmatic enhancements.
