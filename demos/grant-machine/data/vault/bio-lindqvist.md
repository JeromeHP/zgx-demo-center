## Professional Background and Academic Leadership

Marcus Lindqvist serves as the Chair of Mechatronics and Industrial Automation at Harrow Creek Community College (HCCC), a public two-year institution serving four rural counties in the Mid-South. In this capacity, he provides strategic oversight for curriculum development, faculty mentorship, and industry alignment within the college’s technical programs. Dr. Lindqvist holds extensive expertise in bridging the gap between theoretical engineering principles and practical industrial application, a competency that positions him as a critical co-Principal Investigator for the proposed Smart Automation Technician Pathway (SATP).

His academic journey began with a focus on mechanical systems, followed by advanced specialization in control theory and robotics. Over the past decade, Dr. Lindqvist has been instrumental in modernizing HCCC’s technical infrastructure to meet the evolving demands of rural manufacturing. He leads a team of instructors dedicated to integrating Industry 4.0 standards into the classroom, ensuring that students graduate with competencies in programmable logic controllers (PLCs), computer numerical control (CNC) systems, and predictive maintenance protocols.

## Industry Experience and Project History

Prior to his administrative role at HCCC, Dr. Lindqvist spent eight years as a Senior Automation Engineer with several mid-sized manufacturing firms in the region. This industry experience allows him to bring real-world problem-solving scenarios into the curriculum, enhancing student engagement and employability. He has maintained active partnerships with local employers, including Kessler Fluid Systems and Tri-County Packaging Cooperative, both of whom have expressed strong support for the SATP initiative through formal letters of commitment dated August 2026.

Dr. Lindqvist’s leadership was pivotal in the success of HCCC’s prior NSF ATE-funded project, *Mechatronics Pathways for Rural Manufacturers* (Award ID: HCCC-FICT-ATE-2021). Running from September 2021 to August 2025, this initiative aimed to increase workforce readiness in rural areas. Under his co-investigation, the project established three new courses and secured dual-credit agreements with two local high schools. Although the project faced challenges in reaching its target of 120 completers—achieving 84 graduates—it demonstrated a robust 78% placement rate among the 61 respondents who secured employment in relevant fields. This experience has provided Dr. Lindqvist with valuable insights into recruitment strategies, student retention mechanisms, and effective industry collaboration, all of which he now applies to the SATP proposal.

## Role in the Proposed Project

As Co-PI for the three-year Smart Automation Technician Pathway (starting July 2027), Dr. Lindqvist will oversee the technical curriculum design and ensure alignment with employer needs identified by partners such as Northgate Precision Castings. He will collaborate closely with Dean of Workforce and Technical Programs Dr. Renee Okafor and Evaluator Dr. Priya Raman to monitor program outcomes. His goal is to leverage lessons from the previous award to improve completion rates, targeting 150 graduates who are prepared for high-skill roles in the region’s expanding automation sector.
